## Question, chronology and prior method

Previous source studies D06–D09 authenticated original 2026 publisher ZIPs and measured deterministic four-field key correspondence between ANEEL's emergency occurrence and electric interruption files. The frozen D09 study measured **3,609,614 matching distinct full-code literal keys**, of which **3,307,396** had exactly one occurrence and one interruption row. Its documented-width subset had **2,968,556 matching keys**, **2,716,202** with exactly one row in each source. The remaining key multiplicities were already known **before D10**.

D10 was explicitly registered **after** this source/linkage information was known, but **before any D10 source timestamp or feeder/substation pair was inspected**. It asked a narrower observational question: what is the agreement of **printed time intervals and original feeder/substation codes** in these unambiguous 1:1 *textual candidate* pairs? That is not a blind prediction, independently sampled test, newly discovered entity-resolution algorithm or physical restoration intervention.

Both original ANEEL dataset pages describe temporal granularity at the second level, but the current source content alone does not establish common synchronized physical clocks, decision-time availability of every field or independent recording systems. The official original 2026 dictionaries give distinct names for the start/end columns and differing widths for the substation fields. Treat literal string agreement and reported-time overlap as **source-data consistency**, nothing more.

## Frozen interval and code comparison rules

Only one-to-one literal-key groups were evaluated. D10 used unmodified `DthIniOcorrencia` / `DthFimOcorrencia` versus `DatInicioInterrupcao` / `DatFimInterrupcao`; year-first or day-first timestamp formats with seconds, optional fractional seconds up to six digits, and **no implicit timezone conversion**. Calendar-impossible, format-unrecognized, missing or reversed records were not silently repaired: they are **UNKNOWN_TIME**.

For individually complete, non-reversed intervals, two **closed civil-time intervals** were marked **overlapping** if `max(start1,start2) <= min(end1,end2)`, or **disjoint** otherwise. Touching at a boundary was counted separately. This **cannot** tell us whether both records observe one physical power loss or how any repair would be decided.

Both `CodAlimentador` values and both `CodSubestacao` values were compared as **literal original strings**, with **UNKNOWN** when either source was empty, **EQUAL** for two identical nonempty strings and **DIFFERENT** for two nonempty unequal strings. Neither spelling match nor mismatch verifies time-specific energized topology.

### Distinguish missing data from contradictions

Of the **3,307,396** F one-to-one candidate pairs, **3,298,155** have two fully valid non-reversed intervals. Among those, **3,297,487 overlap** as printed and **668 are disjoint**. Thus an overwhelming majority of timestamp-comparable *source-text pairs* have overlapping periods. This does **not** validate the physical pairing: the fields may be derived from related source systems or different parts of one operational incident, and the source linkage field was expressly intended to be related. High agreement can therefore be unsurprising even without independently verified entity truth.

The **9,241** temporally unknown F pairs include **9,241** with an interruption-side missing start/end and **5,799** with a missing occurrence-side start/end. Those reasons overlap and **must not be added**. No unsupported timestamp format, reversed interval or invalid calendar date was reported under the frozen parser in these unique pairs. This says nothing about timestamp quality among unmatched or ambiguous keys.

The **668 valid disjoint intervals are not automatically publication errors**. They are precise disagreements under *our registered closed-interval criterion*. Their real interpretation depends on event definitions, timestamp origin and operational granularity and cannot be decided without further documentation. We did not retrieve or disclose individual cases to explain them, and we did not change the interval criterion post hoc.

The feeder codes agree in **3,288,117** of the F one-to-one text pairs, with **19,279** literal disagreements. Both original feeder fields were nonempty for these candidates. This is a strong syntactic concordance for the already selected text-key cohort, not proof of an energized feeder edge or a confirmed same-incident label.

**Substation comparisons remain dramatically incomplete:** **2,239,820** F candidate pairs contain an empty substation code on at least one side. Only **1,058,781** F pairs have matching nonempty substation strings and **8,795** have differing nonempty strings. This means substation equality cannot independently validate most candidate pairs, regardless of a high time-overlap or feeder-equality percentage.

The F-only long-code one-to-one partition has **591,194** pairs, all of which have valid overlapping or disjoint original time intervals under the frozen parser (591,165 / 29), but **178,146** still lack a substation comparison. No claim is made that long occurrence codes are semantically valid under the dictionary's stated ten-character width.

## Scientific interpretation and required stopping point

The study advanced from exact source-object identity and textual candidate keys to **measured internal time/feeder/substation consistency** on a tightly defined, reproducible one-to-one textual-key cohort. It also exposed critical limitations: **668 disjoint valid source intervals, 19,279 feeder string disagreements, 8,795 substation string disagreements, 2,239,820 unknown substation comparisons, 9,241 unknown time comparisons and 302,218 ambiguous multi-record matching keys**. These numbers refer to partly overlapping error/unknown dimensions and **must not be summed** as independent anomalies.

It has **not** established independently verified physical incident identity, causally observed operator-time features, source completeness, historical switching or feeder energization, available repair alternatives, real CPU/crew decision costs, a causal lookahead-over-greedy benefit, physically meaningful F2 redundancy or a law of relational persistence. Even every pair that simultaneously has overlapping periods and identical feeder/substation strings may reflect correlated data generation rather than independently observed ground truth.

A next **separately registered** D11 would need to audit the **semantic eligibility** of paired event records and the publisher's documentation of event/feeder/substation coding, preferably with an independent incident audit source and explicit handling of one-to-many keys. If no independent source can establish intervention history, the correct research status is **insufficient observational evidence for a physical repair-policy test**. Do not build, tune or declare predictive repairs using post-event durations or end dates as pre-event information.

**Bottom line:** D10 verifies high consistency in certain **published string and civil-interval fields**, within a selected group of same-key records. That is a legitimate bounded data-quality result, not a physical validation of pregeometry or a restoration law.
