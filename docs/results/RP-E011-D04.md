Independent streaming and sorted-key partition counters agreed,
and every one of the 472,399 eligible jobs appeared exactly once
in one partition. The publisher-provided data rows were not
trimmed by wait outcomes or by favorable queues; no forecasting
target errors were inspected in D04. The source was parsed with
strict UTF-8 and original 22-field extended SWF rules, and the
documented byte hash matched D02 and D03 before interpreting rows.

The original file contains long absolute-looking numeric timestamps
and no standard SWF header comments. Generic SWF documentation
often describes submit times relative to the log beginning; that
does **not establish** the timestamp origin of this publisher's
nonstandard extended file. D04 uses only monotonic time ordering
and differences on the file's original clock, without imposing
a calendar offset assumption.

## Limits

A source with 13 actual queues can still be highly unbalanced
over time. D04's minimum two-queue longitudinal coverage was
a **feasibility gate**, not evidence of representativeness across
all queue types. In particular, the subsequent separately
preregistered [P01 forecast](RP-E011-P01.md) found that two queues
accounted for **99.68%** of its future-period target rows.
That P01 negative prediction is **subsequent evidence** and
must never be described as a D04 finding or as a forecast
used to select D04's original rules.

No SWF record of a queued job that never eventually started
can be manufactured from archived started-job history;
right-censoring and scheduler-specific resource/priority policies
remain potential confounders. D04 did not test a scalar
`C=B/(I*tau)`, an actual post-disturbance recovery endpoint,
or a novel theorem. Its only conclusion is that the fixed
**conditional target population** passes the predeclared source
and temporal coverage gates.
