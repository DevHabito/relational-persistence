# RP-U006-P01 — Pre-registered service recovery forecast, executable first result

**First successful original hosted experiment, 17 November 2026:** `RP_U006_EXECUTABLE_STATE_FORECAST_GAIN_WITHIN_HOST`. The experiment is a **single-host TCP software instrumentation and held-out forecasting test**, not a field network, an independent hardware measurement or a discovery about biology, the brain, cancer or fundamental physics.

## Question and link to earlier results

RP-U005 established in a deterministic four-node toy model that repaired connectivity and identical post-repair arrivals can still coexist with different zero-backlog recovery times if previously accumulated work differs. Its arbitrary network-path-to-capacity mapping remains unmeasured. RP-U006 is a different, deliberately narrower operational step: **does measuring accepted-but-uncompleted service work before enabling a real software worker improve the predicted time to two independently monitored empty-service samples?**

This question is already familiar to queueing and service-reliability literature, and our existing RP-E004/E005/E009 studies had already run software service and monitoring experiments. The RP-U006 intervention is **software worker availability** (disabled → enabled), **not a repair of a physical network link**. The monitor has its own OS process, yet reads the same service's reported counters: **this is not a physically independent sensor**. The batch size is also known to the injector, so the 'state' predictor does not discover hidden workload information; this is mainly a **source-instrumented feasibility and calibration study**.

## Independent roles and endpoint audit

Each original trial had a fresh localhost TCP `asyncio` service process with one FIFO worker, and a **separately spawned monitor** repeatedly querying read-only health (accepted, completed, queued, active, available) approximately every 20 ms. The controller/injector in the orchestration process submitted a batch while the worker was unavailable; the service recorded a timestamp of enabling work. The original source files include per-job accept/start/complete records with monotonically ordered service-side timestamps, per-probe time-tagged monitor samples, controller/injector confirmations, configuration, per-trial digest metadata and provenance.

Original source inspection found **15 valid trial groups**, **77 archived files** and **302 monitor samples**. No original job ID was lost, duplicated or completed before availability restoration. The service exited cleanly in all trials. The independent archival check replayed job identity and queue conservation, verified SHA256s of the separate original role logs, recomputed each trial's first pair of consecutive monitor-empty observations, recalculated the calibration from jobs3..12, and recomputed the unmodified registered forecasts and final decision.

The recovery target is **elapsed monotonic-clock seconds from the server-side enable event** to the *second* of two post-enable consecutive monitor health samples with no accepted-but-uncompleted work, separated by at least 15 ms. This is a sample-based service endpoint, **not proof the queue was empty at every instant between probes**.

## Training/calibration and preregistered heldout forecasts

- Healthy calibration observed a median time between consecutive completions of **0.012642039 seconds** over the frozen last nine gaps. The 12 ms requested `asyncio.sleep` is not an observed fixed service duration; the empirical median includes OS/event loop scheduling.
- Six diagnostic recovery durations (seconds, in source order) were approximately **0.136479, 0.241524, 0.342740, 0.136098, 0.238587, 0.340669**. Their frozen median is **0.2400555515 seconds**. This is the **availability-only** forecast, not an artificially zero-time baseline.
- The state-aware forecast, set before recovery is observed, is **(outstanding work at enable) × 0.012642039 seconds + 0.040 seconds**; both coefficients were fixed by the pre-source protocol and its nine-interval correction. There were no post-enable arrivals, and the outstanding count was independently verified against service accept/completion and monitor health. No holdout target was used to alter the baseline, the capacity interval or the 40 ms allowance.

## Why the result is scientifically narrow

The 15 trials are **not independent natural samples**. All use one artificial program and one hosted VM; the batch sizes are fixed, the service schedule is simple, the healthy service time is close to the published 12 ms task delay, **there are zero post-enable arrivals**, and the outstanding count is also known from submission. The comparison therefore shows that **a simple measured state variable is highly useful against a constant baseline in this engineered setting**, not that network topology is insufficient under all full-state models, nor that a relation-only descriptor or a new persistence scalar predicts physical recovery.

The original 2026 RP-U005 deterministic example was not overturned, but neither study independently demonstrates restored two-route network capacity. RP-U006 does **not** identify causal neuronal interactions, psychological identity, cancer stabilization, ecological resilience, physics of emergent time or a physical law. The improved time forecast is an elementary queue-state effect, not mathematical originality.

### Next discriminating step

A more challenging new study would pre-register trials with **new arrivals after repair**, variable service times, independent hardware or external service telemetry, and a much stronger **input-aware predictor without observed backlog** rather than only a constant availability baseline. It should measure how much additional information backlog supplies after prior load and throughput have already been made visible to both forecasting methods. A complete original source/feature lineage and a real functional requirement (work completion/availability, not just recovery of a graph edge) are essential.
