## Motivation and prior results

Original external RP-E010-P01, with 49,995 heldout HPC2N waits, was
`NO_REGISTERED_OBSERVATIONAL_GAIN`. Original RP-E011-P01 with
169,189 heldout CERIT-SC waits was `NO_REGISTERED_QUEUE_GAIN`:
TREND 45,328.01 s MAE versus STATIC 43,953.98 s and a historical
queue/resource-aware median 34,309.43 s. Those results, archived
separately with original data integrity evidence, MUST remain
unchanged. They neither verify nor refute a universal physical law.
RP-F006 is a **new abstract deterministic counterexample**, not an
ex-post explanation proven to be causal in those datasets.

Established prior theory includes:
- Ward Whitt (1999), *Predicting Queueing Delays*, Management Science
  45(6), 870–888, https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.45.6.870:
  queue lengths alone do not exhaust predictive state; current
  service age and remaining service times can help.
- Little (1961), *A Proof for the Queuing Formula: L = lambda W*,
  https://doi.org/10.1287/opre.9.3.383: a stationary average law,
  not a per-arrival deterministic wait identity.
- Parallel batch-job scheduling/backfilling prior literature:
  https://hpc.llnl.gov/banks-jobs/running-jobs/batch-system-primer
  and Murali and Vadhiyar, *Qespera* (2016),
  https://doi.org/10.1002/cpe.3735.

Our counterexample is a **project-specific explicit finite witness
of a classical non-identifiability principle**, not a newly
discovered queuing theorem or physical phenomenon. The mathematical
loss bound follows from the existing triangle inequality.

## Strictly fixed synthetic model

An ideal deterministic, nonpreemptive, one-server FCFS queue with
continuous time measured in synthetic seconds, one job at a time,
and no unregistered arrivals. Three jobs on **the same** queue,
each requesting one processor and exactly the same public walltime
limit in both worlds (500 seconds). No future actual runtime of
a running job is known to a submitted target.

Both worlds contain:

- Historical job H: submitted at -201; started -200; completed -190;
  observed wait 1; actual run 10.
- Running job R: submitted at -31; started -30; requested walltime
  500; observed wait 1.
- Target job T: submitted at 0; requested one processor; same queue,
  same configured walltime in both worlds; target service duration 1.

**World SHORT:** R completes at t=1 (runtime 31);
T starts at 1 and completes at 2. Thus target wait W_SHORT=1.

**World LONG:** R completes at t=100 (runtime 130);
T starts at 100 and completes at 101. Target wait W_LONG=100.

The only varying future fact is R's actual completion time; both
durations are positive and less than the **identical publicly
requested 500-second walltime**, so including the requested limit
does not distinguish the worlds. All submit, start and completion
events **strictly before t=0**, all known job and queue requests,
the running job's age (30 seconds), and target requests are
identical. R is still executing at origin in both worlds.
Do not treat actual duration or future completion as observable.

The project RP-E011-P01 pre-origin observation includes
`q(t-120)=q(-120)=0`, `q(t-60)=q(-60)=0`,
the per-queue prior-start count in `[-3600,-60)` of exactly
`1` (historical H only), historical started-job median
of `1 second`, `B=1/3540 started jobs/s` in that fixed window,
and the same target queue/requested-processor group.
Thus **HISTORY=1**, **STATIC=0**, **TREND=0** in BOTH worlds.
The agreement extends to the **full observable event history up
to (but not including future events after) the target's submission**,
not only these selected summaries.

## Prespecified assertions and falsifiers

1. A Python exact-rational event-driven FCFS replay independently
   checks that each world satisfies the common declared old,
   running and target submission/start/completion schedule;
   overlapping service or FCFS inversion invalidates the witness.
2. An independent finite event-log oracle (not calling the
   simulator's queue-length method) checks binary-count versus
   chronological event-sweep results at -120, -60 and just before
   target origin, plus same known historical starts and requests.
3. World inputs **must** be identical in every available
   pre-origin component; the first differing outcome must be
   future completion of R, and observed target waits must be
   exactly 1 and 100. Failure -> `WITNESS_INVALID`, never adjust
   to a convenient outcome.
4. For **every real deterministic predictor** `f` that takes only
   this shared observation, its output `p` is identical across
   worlds, hence
   `|p-1|+|p-100| >= 99` and
   `max(|p-1|,|p-100|) >= 99/2` seconds.
   Lean 4 must prove this unrestricted real-valued impossibility,
   conditional on the declared shared observation, **without**
   `sorry`, `admit` or `axiom`. The proof is a finite instance
   of triangle inequality, NOT a novel general theorem.
5. Exact rational independent calculation must verify the same
   `99/2` minimax value, attained by constant prediction 101/2,
   and compare the three frozen P01 predictors on both worlds
   without empirical model refitting. The two-world average MAE
   bound of 99/2 **only if** worlds are hypothetically equally
   weighted is a declared synthetic illustration, never an
   estimated population risk.
6. Negative controls: (i) if both world completions are fixed to
   t=1, there is no target contrast; (ii) if the actual future
   completion time is included in the feature input (an invalid
   *oracle* for real prediction), the worlds become distinguishable;
   (iii) changing the target runtime without changing its start
   must not change its wait. All three controls must pass.
7. Any discrepancy between mathematical proof, independent event
   oracles and Python exact calculations -> `WITNESS_INVALID`,
   preserve it, and make no conclusion of observational
   non-identifiability from this particular witness.

The registered positive status is `FINITE_NONIDENTIFIABILITY_VERIFIED`
**only** if every item 1–6 passes, with runnable sources and verified
Lean CI. This is a structural *impossibility of perfect prediction*
for this declared incomplete-information FCFS observation class
and pair of admissible worlds, **not** evidence that all real HPC
waits exhibit this ambiguity or that all statistical prediction is
impossible.
