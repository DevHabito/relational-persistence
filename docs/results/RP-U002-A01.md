## Defined replacement and repair dynamics

For each of original six-node seven-edge
networks A and B, separately remove exactly
ONE of the four internal nodes, each in its
own experiment, never removing source 0
or destination 5. Four independent scenarios
per graph with no post-outcome selection.

Time t=0..5 is dimensionless discrete model
time, not real seconds or plant growth days.

- `t=0`: original six-node system and all its edges.
- `t=1`: permanently remove original internal
  node `v` and ALL of its original incident edges.
- `t=2`: add a **NEW identity** `6+v` and
  reconnect it to precisely ONE original neighbor,
  the smallest numeric neighbor in a predeclared
  ascending sequence.
- `t=3`: reconnect to the second neighbor,
  if present; `t=4` to the third, if present;
  `t=5` to the fourth, if present.
- NO other edges, nodes, losses or controller
  decisions change. The schedule uses a
  disclosed original-network blueprint, not
  hidden future measurements or optimizing
  against test outcomes.
- The matched failure/no-repair control has
  the SAME loss `v` at t=1 but NEVER adds
  a replacement node or new edge.

This policy restores up to ONE original
neighbor-link per repair step. It is NOT
proven a minimal-cost or realistic repair
algorithm; repair and no-repair control
have different later component counts and
costs. No stochastic noise or gradual
adaptation is simulated.

## Registered independent functions

`F1`: at least ONE valid undirected path
from the source node 0 to the destination 5.

`F2`: at least TWO internally vertex-disjoint
source-to-destination paths; strictly stronger
and requiring a different functional guarantee.

Uptime is the exact number of successful
`F` cases among **four separate predetermined
loss scenarios × five after-damage steps
t=1..5 = 20 scenario-steps**. It is NOT
an unbiased estimate of true-world reliability
or population probability.

## Original time-by-time findings

All tables below report **number of the four
separately preregistered loss scenarios**
retaining the respective function in each
toy network. Every source graph/time/removal
pair was checked, and per-scenario results
are in the first archived JSON.

### F1: at least one remaining source-target path

| Graph and policy | t0 | t1 | t2 | t3 | t4 | t5 | successful t1..5 /20 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| A repaired | 4 | 4 | 4 | 4 | 4 | 4 | **20/20** |
| A no repair | 4 | 4 | 4 | 4 | 4 | 4 | **20/20** |
| B repaired | 4 | 3 | 3 | 3 | 4 | 4 | **17/20** |
| B no repair | 4 | 3 | 3 | 3 | 3 | 3 | **15/20** |

The original B graph has one single-vertex
bottleneck at `v=2`. At t=1 it loses
the terminal path. At t=2 it ALREADY has
the original SIX components once again
(because new identity 8 replaced old
identity 2), yet it still lacks the
declared path. The new node has only
one original-neighbor link; at t=3
it has two, STILL no path; at t=4
adding its third old-neighbor link
restores F1. The fourth is added
at t=5. This is a conditional example
of **component-count recovery preceding
FUNCTION recovery**, not the introduction
of a new scientific law.

The repair policy improves B F1 uptime
by exactly **2/20 scenario-steps** over
a fixed no-repair scenario but yields
**zero F1 gain** on network A because
network A never lost F1 from a
single registered deletion.

### F2: two internally vertex-disjoint terminal paths

| Graph and policy | t0 | t1 | t2 | t3 | t4 | t5 | successful t1..5 /20 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| A repaired | 4 | 0 | 0 | 3 | 4 | 4 | **11/20** |
| A no repair | 4 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | **0/20** |
| B repaired | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | **0/20** |
| B no repair | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | **0/20** |

Network A's function F2 was initially
present, lost for all four single
node deletions, regained in THREE
scenarios by t=3, and regained in
all four by t=4. The t=3 unrepaired
F2 case is the original failure
of node 3, whose third original
neighbor-link is required to restore
the second independent route.

Network B cannot recover F2 via
this particular repair policy
because it NEVER satisfied F2
in the original t=0 graph.
F2 is NOT interchangeable with F1.
Reporting only repaired connectivity
would therefore exaggerate recovery
of the stronger function.

## Validation details and negative controls

- State constructor A: independent
  direct closed-form original-edge
  filtering plus prefix of originally
  registered neighbor attachments.
- State constructor B: sequential
  application of original node-loss,
  new-identity addition and one
  incremental link event per tick.
- F1 oracle A: independent adjacency
  BFS on present components.
- F1 oracle B: independent exhaustive
  enumeration of simple terminal paths.
- F2 oracle A: disjoint interiors of
  enumerated simple path pairs.
- F2 oracle B: F1 after deletion of
  EACH individual surviving nonterminal
  component (Menger's classical
  vertex-connectivity principle, scoped
  to models without a direct 0-5 edge).
- Adversarial tests reject missing/
  changed source topologies, a former
  failed identity reappearing, dangling
  edges, exceeding one restored link
  per tick, wrong restoration order,
  future model time, incorrect repaired
  status, disagreement of independent
  oracles, false registered predicted
  counts and overwriting original
  outputs.
- The small additional
  [Lean 4 module](../../RelationalPersistence/TurnoverRepair.lean)
  proves by finite `decide` over
  explicit B-node-2 before/after
  edge sets that the connectivity
  remains false at t1/t2/t3 and
  becomes true at t4/t5. It also
  checks that new-identity t2 has
  exactly six present nodes while
  the function remains absent.
  This is **not** a kernel proof
  of the Python source replay
  or all possible graphs/costs.
- Registered NEGATIVE findings
  were NOT deleted: A gains zero
  F1 uptime, B gains zero F2
  uptime, and B's F1 benefit
  is only two of twenty registered
  scenario-step observations.

No ecological time measurements
or statistical confidence intervals
exist in this experiment.

## Interpretation and prior work

The exercise illustrates the already
well-established distinction between
**structural restoration** (a new
node exists), **relational restoration**
(the requisite connections exist)
and **task-specific functional
restoration** (the chosen terminal
service criterion holds).

Known prior scientific and
mathematical context includes:

- K. Menger (1927) classical theorem
  on graph vertex cuts and
  internally vertex-disjoint routes.
- C. S. Holling (1973),
  [*Resilience and Stability of Ecological Systems*](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev.es.04.110173.000245).
- E. Desjardins et al. (2015),
  [*Promoting Resilience*](https://doi.org/10.1086/681439).
- J. Gao, B. Barzel and A.-L.
  Barabási (2016),
  [*Universal resilience patterns
  in complex networks*](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature16948)
  and published
  [erratum](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature18019).

**New project artifact:** transparently
frozen finite time-indexed identity-turnover
policy, exact two-function and two-policy
adversarial contrasts for EVERY registered
case, independent source-state and
path-oracle checks, finite Lean

negative-inclusive report.
