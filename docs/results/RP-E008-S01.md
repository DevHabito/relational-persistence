## Exact frozen comparison

There were three arrival classes (`uniform`,
`early_burst`, `late_burst`) crossed with three
prechange-service patterns (`constant`, `mixed`,
`high_variance`) and eight frozen random seeds,
for **72 deterministic cases**. Cases with replicate
IDs 0–3 were diagnostic; IDs 4–7 were the
**36 primary held-out simulation outcomes**.
The random inputs use fixed source seeds; this
is a controlled mathematical construction, not a
probability sample of real hardware.

For an FCFS backlog observed at t=925 and 975 ms,
and predeclared B=1000/18 work/s, both predictors
are **unfitted**:

```text
Yhat_static = q_975/(2*B)
v = (q_975-q_925)/0.050 seconds
Yhat_trend = max(0, q_975 + v*0.025)/(2*B).
```

Recovery Y was the first *sampled* idle after
t=1000 ms, with 500-ms sampled-idle confirmation.
True last completion was also computed independently,
and the positive sampled-vs-true gap remained
strictly below 25 ms; simulation output must not
be passed off as a direct physical recovery measurement.

| Held-out result, 36 cases | Exact rational MAE (s) | Decimal MAE (ms) |
|---|---:|---:|
| Static queue snapshot | `533/18000` | 29.6111 |
| Two-sample trend | `173/14400` | 12.0139 |

**Improvement: 59.43%.** The exact rational test
`MAE_trend ≤ (9/10) × MAE_static` passed the frozen
10% reduction threshold. The full diagnostic
block and held-out aggregate are included in the
original JSON; no model parameters were tuned on
those 36 held-out outcomes.

## The important negative subgroup

**The trend did not dominate pointwise.**
It won in **20** of 36 held-out cases and lost
in **16**. One fixed arrival regime contradicted
a stronger claim of universally positive effects:

| Held-out arrival regime | Static MAE (ms) | Trend MAE (ms) | Interpretation |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Uniform | 22.3333 | 13.4167 | Trend lower MAE |
| Early burst | **5.0000** | **12.1250** | **Static lower MAE** |
| Late burst | 61.5000 | 10.5000 | Trend much lower MAE |

The positive aggregate was therefore driven
substantially by better prediction of late-arrival
bursts. The frozen early-burst counterexample
must be retained, not removed as an "outlier".
The mixed and high-variance service patterns
and per-trial deviations are also preserved in
the machine-readable summary.

The result shows that the two-sample trend rule
can **help or harm** prediction depending on
how arrivals are distributed in time, even when
both event engines agree exactly on the modeled
dynamics. The aggregate 59.43% is the frozen
study score, not a population-generalizable
effect estimate or a universal performance bound.

## Verification and scientific boundaries

The code [`rp_e008_independent_des.py`](../../experiments/rp_e008_independent_des.py)
uses two separate implementations:

- **Oracle A:** exact max-plus FCFS completion
  recursion and backlog counts.
- **Oracle B:** explicit chronological event
  heap, waiting FIFO, active job and completion
  calendar with an independently computed
  sampled event-status stream.

Integer-time operations and all registered
MAE decisions use exact Python `Fraction` arithmetic.
Decimal values are only labeled approximations
for the report.

The permanent regression test
[`test_rp_e008_first_run_archive.py`](../../tests/test_rp_e008_first_run_archive.py)
checks the complete 74-file manifest, independently
reexecutes the identical deterministic model to
verify each source/event record, and requires
both the global positive result and the early-burst
negative subgroup to remain unchanged. A corrupt
source file cannot match its recorded digest.

**Model verification is not empirical model
validation.** The arrival schedules, task service
times, simulated calibration B, end-to-end queue
observations and exact drain outcome are authored
mathematical inputs and outputs. No physical
hardware, independent sensor, live alternative
service implementation, statistical sampling
inference, or new Lean theorem is supplied.
This does not validate `C=B/(I*tau)` or overturn
RP-E006's negative predictor result.

Background: the distinction between verifying
and validating discrete-event models is covered
in [simulation modeling literature](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6001262/).
A separate real service with independently measured
arrivals, service times and recovery labels would
be the next **empirical** transfer test, to be
registered before collecting its data.
