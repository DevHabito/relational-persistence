## What I set out to test

In RP-U003, giving every repair policy the same edge budget was not enough to rule out differences in available information. Before writing the present implementation, I froze a new [RP-U004 protocol](../preregistration/RP-U004-P01.md) at commit `186d6fc5d8cc6451fca63abb5f6ef4f2029a3d7b`. I specified a new seven-vertex/eight-edge family, selected graphs by labeled degree vectors without inspecting their outcomes, gave every strategy the same complete F1/F2 subset-function table, and preregistered a directional comparison against a stronger F2-aware greedy baseline.

The outcomes were not known when the protocol and thresholds were fixed. The earlier RP-U003 results, including its important aggregate F1 tie against GREEDY1, were already known. I do not treat this as independent confirmation of an unseen graph-theory hypothesis.

## Source population and experimental unit

I enumerated every labeled connected simple graph on vertices `0..6` with exactly eight edges, source/target degrees two, and no direct `(0,6)` link. Two separately coded enumerators were required to agree. The unfiltered construction was completely determined before scoring.

| Exact source quantity | First original outcome |
|---|---:|
| Accepted labeled graphs | 18,140 |
| Distinct labeled degree-vector strata | 305 |
| Excluded singleton-degree strata | 20 |
| Retained paired degree-vector strata | 285 |
| Graphs selected (first and last per retained stratum) | 570 |
| Original internal-node failure scenarios | 2,850 |
| Post-failure ticks per scenario | 6 |
| Scenario-ticks per policy | 17,100 |
| Complete primary F1/F2 truth-table entries evaluated | 25,040 |
| Selected original graphs with F2 already present before damage | 142 of 570 |

The selected graphs are a deterministic **subset** of the 18,140 admissible labeled graphs, not a blind representative sample of arbitrary networks. I did not independently sample 2,850 real networks: each selected topology contributes five related failure scenarios, and many graphs are related by label symmetries.

**A crucial negative baseline:** 428 of the 570 selected original graphs lack the stronger F2 function even before any failure. Their 2,140 loss scenarios have F2 total zero under both compared strategies. F2 gains are concentrated in the remaining **710 original-F2-present scenarios**. That distinction matters more than the raw 17,100 denominator when interpreting the F2 effect.

## Registered result — all policies

F1 is at least one source-to-target path. F2 is at least two internally vertex-disjoint source-to-target paths. Both are Boolean functions of a synthetic graph state, not observed clinical, ecological, industrial or aerospace performance.

| Frozen strategy | F1 successful case-ticks / 17,100 | F2 successful case-ticks / 17,100 |
|---|---:|---:|
| ASC | 15,695 | 2,877 |
| DEGREE | 15,825 | 3,106 |
| GREEDY_F1 | 16,046 | 3,286 |
| GREEDY_F2 | 16,046 | 3,286 |
| LOOKAHEAD2_F1 | 16,052 | 3,386 |
| LOOKAHEAD2_F2 | 16,052 | 3,386 |
| Per-scenario offline full-permutation maximum, summed | 16,052 | 3,386 |

**Primary H1 passed:** F2-aware two-step lookahead returned 3,386 successful F2 case-ticks, compared with 3,286 for the F2-aware one-step greedy rule. The exact difference is **100**. At the full-scenario level, lookahead won **66** cases, tied **2,784**, and lost **zero**. Within the 710 scenarios starting from an F2-capable graph, these were 66 wins, 644 ties and zero losses; the other 2,140 cases were zero-F2 ties.

**Registered secondary H2 also passed arithmetically:** the F1 comparison was 16,052 against 16,046, with six scenario wins, 2,844 ties and zero losses. I do **not** present H2 as independent corroboration, for the mathematical reason below.

Neither improvement is an observed probability, a stochastic estimate or a claim about independent random systems. The fixed selected family had all its cases enumerated exactly. A numerical effect over scenario-ticks has no warrant as a general effectiveness percentage in physical networks.

## One important thing the policy names obscured

The benchmark specified six named strategies, but on this Boolean task it actually evaluated only **four distinct repair-decision rules**. Across every recorded one of the 2,850 cases, the original `GREEDY_F1` and `GREEDY_F2` **selected identical repair orders**. The same was true for `LOOKAHEAD2_F1` and `LOOKAHEAD2_F2`.

This is not an unexplained statistical coincidence. Since F2 implies F1, the only admissible Boolean score pairs are

```text
(F1,F2) = (0,0), (1,0), (1,1).
```

Sorting these pairs lexicographically as `(F1,F2)` or as `(F2,F1)` gives the **same strict ranking**. Each of the two policies uses that ranking with the same label tie-break. The same equivalence holds for comparing the lookahead's best next-step score before the immediate score. Consequently the F1-first and F2-first labels do not provide different choices under the declared decision mechanism.

The source code remained faithful to the protocol and the original numerical results remain valid. What changes is their interpretation: the two objective-order variants are redundant controls. H2 does not supply a second independent optimization effect. I will not call that objective comparison two independent confirmations.

The next formal task is to prove the Boolean ranking-equivalence statement in Lean 4, explicitly separating that small mathematical lemma from the already completed 2,850-case computation. No such Lean theorem is claimed in this first report.

## Offline bounds, work and negative controls

The program enumerated all admissible repair-neighbor permutations for each case and separately maximized integrated F1 and F2 over the complete frozen horizon. The sum of the case-level F1 maxima is **16,052**; the F2 maxima sum to **3,386**. Both LOOKAHEAD2 labels achieve these respective maxima **on the selected finite cases**. They do not have zero regret in arbitrary graphs, information settings or measured restoration problems.

| Frozen strategy class | Recorded decision/table lookups | Recorded candidate comparisons |
|---|---:|---:|
| ASC / DEGREE (each) | 14,250 | 0 |
| Either GREEDY variant (each) | 28,450 | 14,200 |
| Either LOOKAHEAD2 variant (each) | 53,410 | 14,200 |

These are exact **instrumented model operations**, not CPU time or monetary costs. The lookahead rules perform substantially more function lookups than the greedy rules despite identical repaired-edge schedules and a common precomputed table. I cannot claim equivalent decision-time computation.

The fixed cyclic relabeling stress test also found changed function totals for some weaker strategies (for example, ASC F1 in 148 scenarios; GREEDY F2 in 81), while neither LOOKAHEAD2 variant showed a changed total under that **one registered cyclic permutation**. This is not an invariance proof for all relabelings or graph sizes. The complete per-case adverse and tie records are retained in the immutable original JSON.

I also ran a separate Python **standard-library-only replay** which did not import the U004 experiment's source. It independently enumerated the complete 18,140-graph accepted family using a union-find connectivity criterion, reproduced the 285 matched-degree pairs and their family hash, rebuilt the F1/F2 truth tables with explicit simple-path and vertex-deletion oracles, recalculated all repair choices and full-permutation maxima, checked every one of the 2,850 source-case records, and reproduced the cyclic-relabeling outcomes, operation counts and aggregate scores. No disagreement was detected.

This is meaningful independent *implementation-level* cross-checking under the **same synthetic model assumptions**. It is not independent empirical replication, and the source code of the original experiment is not itself proved correct in Lean. The replay must remain inspectable and reproducible rather than being summarized merely as a green check.

## Existing literature and what is not original here

My [RP-U004 prior-art review](../literature/RP-U004-PRIOR-ART.md) identifies related research by **Fuyu Hu and colleagues (2016)**, **Novella Bartolini and colleagues (2017)**, **Igor Averbakh and Jordi Pereira (2018)**, **Fenghua Wang and colleagues (2023)**, and **Abigail J. Crocker and Stephen D. Boyles (2025)**. Those publications already study repair scheduling, greedy and lookahead heuristics, and constrained network recovery. Menger's vertex-disjoint-path theorem is substantially older.

The project-specific contribution here is a preregistered, finite, source-complete **within-its-declared-selection-rule** benchmark, including recorded negative and neutral cases, exact reference bounds, source hashes, a common information table and an independent replay. It does not invent network restoration, two-step greedy search, two-path connectivity, or a resilience law.

## What I still cannot say

- These graph ticks are not real seconds, and unit repair-edge costs are not labor, computation, money or hardware resources.
- There are no real power flows, vehicle traffic constraints, patient outcomes, organisms, thermal behavior, spacecraft components or measured disturbances in this model.
- Source/target connectivity and vertex-disjoint paths are only two selected graph observables; they do not define universal system survival.
- The reported matched-degree selection is not an independent natural population, and 2,850 related cases are not 2,850 independent scientific experiments.
- Same knowledge of the original graph is not the same as a certified available-in-time measurement in an unknown real system.
- I have not measured CPU times, resource-use confidence intervals, external forecast gains, or new physical constants here.
- No general lookahead optimality theorem or PMFF/RZS-to-physical-observable bridge has been established.
- The original negative external RP-E010/RP-E011 forecasting results have not been revised.
