# RP-E009-P01 — First prospective executable TCP queue transfer

**Original registered decision: `PASS_EXECUTABLE_TRANSFER`.**

The prospective two-sample backlog forecast exceeded the frozen
10% held-out MAE advantage threshold on a separately implemented
**executable TCP/asyncio queue service**. These are
**real hosted software event timings**, not a physical
law, independent hardware, natural population or an
original discovery in queueing theory.

## Different service and measured observables

This was a separate executable service process
built from standard Python asyncio networking
and FIFO queue primitives rather than a replay
of the previous HTTP/threaded server. The driver
submitted real TCP commands; the server recorded
individual original accepted/start/complete events
under one worker and the client independently
sampled `queued`, `active`, `accepted`,
and `completed` counters with monotonic timestamps.

Each fresh server calibrated pre-intervention B
using **60** prechange tasks cycling through nominal
8/12/16-ms durations. The resulting B is a
**local client-observed completion throughput**:
it includes the service system and measurement
overheads and is not an intrinsic constant of
nature. A service-side recorded factor-two
speed change happened before each intervention;
no causal post-onset tau was inferred.

Three accepted workload targets (40,70,100)
were combined with uniform, early-burst and
late-burst schedules. The original requests'
**actual** sends, service accept times and client
acks are archived; the configured minimum
spacing was not substituted for measured time.

The recovery endpoint `Y` is the first sampled
post-injection zero-backlog, zero-active reading,
confirmed with at least 500 ms of further
sampled idleness, using the original complete
sample stream. Two separately implemented
sample-group and streaming recovery algorithms
agreed on the original source. The service's
individual event timestamps independently
verified each sampled queue state. **No
unobserved continuous-time inactivity** is
certified between probes.

## Frozen predictors and observed MAE

At injector end, only the last two received
status measurements from before or at the
forecast origin entered the estimates.
Both models were predeclared, **unfitted**
and used the same measured B:

```text
Yhat_static = q_last/(2B)
v = (q_last-q_prev)/(t_last-t_prev)
Yhat_trend = max(0,q_last+v*(end-t_last))/(2B)
```

| Held-out predictor (n=9) | MAE (seconds) | MAE (ms) | Status |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Two-sample temporal queue trend | **0.0105265582** | **10.527** | Primary prospective candidate |
| Static latest backlog | 0.0202013382 | 20.201 | Primary comparator |
| Diagnostic-only mean | 0.0677070927 | 67.707 | Secondary control |

The **47.8918%** held-out MAE reduction was
greater than the frozen **10%** requirement.
The trend had a lower error in **7 of 9**
held-out trials, tied in two, and lost in
zero. The registered result was therefore
`PASS_EXECUTABLE_TRANSFER`, not a
post-hoc threshold choice.

## Prespecified arrival-regime breakdown

| Original arrival schedule | Static MAE (ms) | Trend MAE (ms) | Trend reduction |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Uniform | 16.075 | 8.565 | 46.72% |
| Early burst | 14.049 | 12.213 | 13.07% |
| Late burst | 30.479 | 10.802 | 64.56% |

These are **three cases per arrival regime**
and do not support population effect bounds.
The early-burst gain was the smallest.
Notably, RP-E008's **mathematical simulator**
found the opposite direction for its
early-burst regime. RP-E008 and RP-E009
have different event scheduling,
service-time implementations, clocks
and realized arrivals, so they are
**not a controlled paired comparison**.
The discrepancy is a warning against
universal trend-superiority claims,
not grounds to discard either record.

The full original evaluator includes all nine
individual samples, measured B and injection
durations, pre-end queue observations,
two predictions and absolute errors without
report rounding. There was no fitting on
held-out labels or reuse of RP-E007 data as
new observations.

## Integrity checks and scope

The independently authored
[`rp_e009_evaluate.py`](../../experiments/rp_e009_evaluate.py)
reads source bytes and recomputes events,
state, recovery, B and both forecasts.
The [original archive regression](../../tests/test_rp_e009_first_run_archive.py)
checks the entire 131-file SHA-256 inventory,
reruns the registered event audit and evaluation
without starting a service, and rejects
deliberate tampering with original server
events. These checks test evidence
reproducibility, not external sensor independence.

**What is supported:** the same unfitted
classical temporal queue estimate improved
over a static backlog approximation in
another executable software implementation,
under this precise design and finite
hosted sample.

**What is not supported:** any universal
relational persistence law, the predictive
validity of C=B/(I*tau), a claim that the
two software systems are independent
physical instruments, applicability
to arbitrary queues or a statistically
powered causal effect across deployments.
The host, language, and assumptions
remain restricted.

Prior methods and engineering primitives:
[Python asyncio queues](https://docs.python.org/3/library/asyncio-queue.html),
[asyncio stream servers](https://docs.python.org/3/library/asyncio-stream.html),
and the queue-estimation prior-work ledger.
A stronger future transfer must change
not just source code but environment,
arrival mechanisms and measurement
provenance, with a fresh preregistered
test and independent status checks.
