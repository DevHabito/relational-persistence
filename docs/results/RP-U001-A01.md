# RP-U001-A01 — Functional continuity under one-step component loss

**Original first-run verdict: `FINITE_FUNCTION_CONTINUITY_CONTRAST_VERIFIED`.**

This is the FIRST **Functional Continuity** track experiment,
motivated by a discussion of natural forest canopy gaps and
component replacement. It tests only a fully specified
finite mathematical model of two tiny networks. The experiment
contains **NO** ecological observation, biological turnover,
replacement, adaptation, regrowth or time series, and therefore
neither proves nor suggests empirical confirmation of a
universal law across ecological, medical and technological systems.

## System, functional criterion and fixed exhaustive subset design

Six artificial nodes `0..5`; protected fixed terminals
`s=0` and `t=5`; exactly four removable internal
nodes `1,2,3,4`. All edges are undirected.
Both graphs contain exactly **seven edges**,
are initially connected between terminals
and have source/target terminal degree **two**.
They are NOT claimed to match all node degrees
or post-deletion edge counts.

- A edges:
  `(0,1),(1,2),(2,5),(0,3),(3,4),(4,5),(1,3)`.
- B edges:
  `(0,1),(0,3),(1,2),(3,2),(2,5),(2,4),(4,5)`.

In A, paths `0-1-2-5` and `0-3-4-5`
are internally vertex-disjoint.
In B every terminal route crosses node 2.

A fixed removal set deletes ALL its nodes and
incident edges permanently. **No repair is
permitted.** The base operational function
`F(G,S)=1` iff `0` remains linked to `5`.
All `2^4=16` subsets of the four removable
nodes are enumerated for EACH graph, and each
subset is validated by two independently
implemented algorithms: adjacency-BFS and
self-avoiding DFS path enumeration. The original
report stores every test's Boolean result.
A fixed internal-node relabeling
permutation is checked case-by-case.

These are **exact fractions of a finite enumeration**,
NOT failure probabilities learned from a
real population of graphs, forest clearings or
patient-care systems. In particular, "1/3"
describes exactly 2 of the 6 registered
two-node-removal choices.

## Exact original findings

| Removed internal components `k` | Exhaustive cases | Network A retains at least one route | Network B retains at least one route |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | 1 | **1/1** | **1/1** |
| 1 | 4 | **4/4** | **3/4** |
| 2 | 6 | **2/6** | **2/6** |
| 3 | 4 | **0/4** | **0/4** |
| 4 | 1 | **0/1** | **0/1** |

Both networks have the same vertex survival fraction
`(6-k)/6` for a fixed `k`, yet at `k=1`
they need not maintain the same connectivity
function. The observed difference is expected
from their distinct interior vertex connectivity:
a single internal B bottleneck `2` separates
the terminals, whereas A has two independent
routes. This is an illustration of CLASSICAL
vertex cuts, not a new graph-theoretic theorem.

At `k=2`, **B ties A**, matching the explicitly
preregistered negative control and defeating the
stronger statement that A strictly dominates
for every removal magnitude. At `k=3` and
`k=4` neither retains the function.

An additional preregistered task-definition
control changes the operational function to
`F2(G,S)=1` iff TWO internally vertex-disjoint
routes remain. At `k=0`, A satisfies F2, B
does not. After ANY single-component loss,
neither graph satisfies F2. Therefore functional
continuity is not a single property independent
of a **specific required function**.

## Formal and independent computation evidence

- Python oracle A: adjacency-based breadth-first
  reachability after node loss.
- Python oracle B: separately built self-avoiding
  path enumeration, not BFS rerun.
- All 16 original subsets per graph, all
  `k=0..4` and the relabeled counterparts
  were checked against the predeclared vector
  `A=[1,4,2,0,0]`, `B=[1,3,2,0,0]`.
- A new finite
  [Lean 4 module](../../RelationalPersistence/FunctionalContinuity.lean)
  defines both exact edge lists and an explicit,
  bounded Boolean reachability algorithm. Its
  `by decide` theorems certify that ALL FOUR
  single-vertex-loss cases in A preserve the
  Boolean function, and precisely three do so
  in B, with deletion of node 2 failing.
  The certificate also checks initial
  connectivity.
- Lean 4 proves that precise defined finite
  Boolean calculation, not an external
  equivalence theorem about the separate Python
  DFS/BFS or all natural networks.
- Adversarial checks reject corrupted edges,
  attempts to remove protected terminals,
  source graph size/degree mismatch, disagreement
  of reachability oracles, changed registered
  expected counts, and attempts to overwrite the
  original study output.

There were **no field measurements, stochastic
confidence intervals or new physical variables**.
The model does not simulate ecological
facilitation, succession, growth or renewal.
A network that retains 0-to-5 reachability after
a deletion need NOT retain service quality,
bandwidth or other useful functions.

## How this fits the existing Relational Persistence work

The original research already demonstrated
specific failures of scalar summaries to
determine model dynamics (RP-001, RP-F003,
RP-F004, RP-F005), and constrained observability
of queue-wait forecasts (RP-F006/F007).
The new track asks a DIFFERENT, explicitly
operational question: how to formalize
continuity of function when the IDENTITY
of retained components changes.

This first finite experiment measures ONLY
failure tolerance with permanent removals.
It does not yet study actual replacement or
dynamic reorganization. Its paired graphs
match only coarse structure (size, edge count,
terminal degree) rather than identical
internal degree sequences or dynamical
parameters. In addition, identical aggregate
single-loss performance need not imply the
same service function under other task definitions.
These boundaries are deliberately preserved.

The prior **negative observational results**
of RP-E010-P01 and RP-E011-P01 remain exactly
as originally reported. RP-U001 offers no
evidence that the old quotient
`C=B/(I*tau)` is an ecological law.
The project MUST NOT interpret mere
network redundancy as a discovered universal
system property.

## Scientific originality / prior literature

- C. S. Holling (1973),
  [*Resilience and Stability of Ecological Systems*](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev.es.04.110173.000245).
  The conceptual distinction between
  resilience, survival and maintenance
  of relations far predates this study.
- E. Desjardins, G. Barker, Z. Lindo,
  C. Dieleman & A. C. Dussault (2015),
  [*Promoting Resilience*](https://doi.org/10.1086/681439).
  Reviews functional redundancy,
  response diversity and modularity.
- J. Gao, B. Barzel & A.-L. Barabási
  (2016), [*Universal resilience patterns
  in complex networks*](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature16948)
  and its [published erratum](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature18019).
  This is PRIOR multiscale network
  resilience literature, with specified
  assumptions; it does not imply that
  this project's toy graph comparison
  is new theory.
- K. Menger (1927), classical vertex
  connectivity / internally disjoint
  path theorem. See
  [Diestel, *Graph Theory*](https://www.cs.unibo.it/babaoglu/courses/cas00-01/tutorials/GraphTheory.pdf),
  section on Menger's theorem.

**Project-specific contribution:** one transparently
precommitted, fully enumerated six-node
function-definition/structural-indistinguishability
contrast, an explicit counterexample to
damage-level universal strict advantage,
two independent finite path oracles,
case-invariant relabeling check, model-specific
Lean 4 executable certificate and immutable
first original report. The underlying
graph-theory fact is already known and the
specific finite calculation does not, by
itself, demonstrate novel scientific value.

## A credible next test, not yet conducted

Separately preregister an explicit discrete-time
**node-turnover with bounded reconfiguration**
process: specified removals, additions,
edge-formation rules, costs and delays, with
a time-indexed functional continuity target.
Compare exact alternatives with a standard
graph connectivity / disjoint-path baseline
and a strictly weaker size-only model.
An actual ecological test would further
need independent field time-series, a
specific functional endpoint, appropriate
mechanistic ecological controls, no
future-time leakage and prospective
heldout sites. Under either plan, negative
conclusions are first-class outcomes.
