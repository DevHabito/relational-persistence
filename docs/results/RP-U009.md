# RP-U009 — MetroPT-3 physical compressor maintenance-window source feasibility

**17 November 2026. Final source-status: P01 INVALID; D01 BINARY TEXT CODING VERIFIED; P02 INVALID FOR INSUFFICIENT TEMPORAL COVERAGE. No maintenance recovery outcome was eligible.**

## Why the new question mattered

RP-U005–U007 used simulated graph/queue workloads or a worker in our own hosted software; U007 had a preregistered negative incremental-forecast finding. RP-U008 measured two separate protocols at one residential collector after four previously described outages, but without independent physical intervention timestamps. To move toward an external **physical asset**, RP-U009 examined MetroPT-3, pressure and motor-current telemetry from a real railway air compressor paired with publicly reported operator maintenance dates. This is useful source engineering but not independent physical repair verification.

The primary source is Narjes Davari et al., **MetroPT-3**, UCI Machine Learning Repository dataset 791, DOI https://doi.org/10.24432/C5VW3R. Background: Veloso, Ribeiro, Gama and Pereira (2022), *The MetroPT dataset for predictive maintenance*, DOI https://doi.org/10.1038/s41597-022-01877-3. The company failure report table was known before this research: four failures were printed, but the May29–May30 fault was paired with a logically inconsistent April30 maintenance entry; the April18 fault had no unambiguous reported repair. We transparently retained only the two later dated maintenance anchors, June8 at 16:00 and July16 at 00:00, **before downloading source records**. This was an eligibility decision based on report chronology, not a random or representative sample. Sensor timestamps are **naive clock labels**, without verified timezone/cross-device synchronization.

## Three distinct stages, without rewriting history

### P01 — original first preregistered source (invalid)

The full frozen P01 protocol was committed **`2c36bd9b84ac664798e544f24b3617c1f7b5a258`**, before opening source values. It froze exactly two maintenance dates and four windows: from 24h before to 2h before each anchor, and 2h after to 24h after it. Each window required >=30 source records spanning **at least six distinct hourly buckets**. The principal descriptive outcome would be the before-minus-after fraction of the physical reservoir-pressure sensor below **7 bar**, accompanied by motor current >5A and a distinct low-pressure-switch diagnostic. Both events needed strictly positive improvement in the low-pressure fraction; no fitted threshold or post-source window adjustment was allowed.

## Scientific conclusion: a failed observational eligibility test, not a physical recovery result

The actual external compressor telemetry is useful, but our specific two-anchor, four-window study **was not eligible**. Both the released **sample cadence** (median 10 seconds) and the **coverage of the chosen windows** matter. A pre-maintenance window with 600 readings over a nominal 22-hour span is not equivalent to a near-continuously observed 22-hour baseline; it may reflect the train's operating/sampling schedule. Without the frozen minimum six observed hours, comparing before/after pressure can confound maintenance with the observation process. The actual number of distinct covered hours beyond “fewer than six” was not logged, so we do not invent it.

Even with perfect window coverage, the published maintenance report is not a verified precise completion timestamp, the sensor's timezone is not independently established relative to it, sensor values are sampled on one train, and pressure alone is not a measured delivered-work result. There is no causal basis to attribute a change to maintenance. In particular **we have not demonstrated restoration of physical function, a new relational quantity, recovery in cancer or the nervous system, or a law of persistence.**

This failure changes the next research question. Before spending more effort on forecasting and formal methods, a source should pass independent eligibility gates for (1) verified intervention vs source timestamp alignment; (2) sufficient *pre- and post-* observation coverage with no favorable-event selection; (3) an independently recorded functional output or at least a verified process-performance proxy; and (4) enough independent incidents to justify population analysis. DAYPSCI (2026), https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dib.2026.113146, offers controlled external fault injection on industrial PLC hardware, but its original file access and independently reconciled sensor/network timestamps still need verification. Another software toy is not an adequate substitute.
