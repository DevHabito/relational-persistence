# RP-U007-P01 — Does a directly observed backlog improve a capacity- and arrival-aware recovery forecast?

**Preregistration: 17 November 2026. This commit must contain ONLY this protocol. No U007 original source measurements, outcomes, code, tests, or probes have been inspected or executed.**

## Why this is a harder test than U006

RP-U006 compared a predictor using measured outstanding work with a baseline that knew the restored worker was available but otherwise forecast a constant recovery duration. A positive 8/8 first held-out software result with 88% MAE reduction was valid under its own frozen rules, but the injector had already supplied every job in the batch: the state observation was essentially a known workload variable. It does not show unique physiological or relational information.

RP-U007 therefore makes the **input-only comparator substantially more informative**. It receives the exact accepted initial job count, timestamped early arrivals, the fixed future-arrival schedule, the available worker status, the elapsed time since enable, and an independently calibrated service interval for the **correct** hidden-but-revealed regime. The state-aware challenger receives all of the same information **plus just one directly monitored pre-landmark outstanding count**. The question is whether that additional live observation improves future recovery forecasts beyond an input-aware and capacity-aware queue prediction.

The chosen comparison is a familiar queueing/forecasting problem, **not an invention**. See R. Ibrahim & W. Whitt (2008), *Real-Time Delay Estimation Based on Delay History*, Manufacturing & Service Operations Management, https://doi.org/10.1287/msom.1080.0223; Ibrahim & Whitt (2011), *Wait-Time Predictors for Customer Service Systems with Time-Varying Demand and Capacity*, Operations Research, https://doi.org/10.1287/opre.1110.0974; and *Workload of Queueing Systems with Autocorrelated Service Times* (2025), Entropy 27(3):272, https://doi.org/10.3390/e27030272. The Python worker, timing and role protocols build on this repository's documented RP-E009/RP-U006, rather than claiming any first software queue experiment.

## Physical and measurement scope

A new program runs on **one GitHub-hosted Ubuntu 24.04 VM**. A TCP server process owns a FIFO single worker that cannot consume tasks until an explicit enable event. Job nominal durations are drawn by the server using **independent operating-system randomness**, with three registered distributions:
- **fast:** uniform choice among 6, 9, 12 ms requested \`asyncio.sleep\`;
- **standard:** uniform choice among 8, 12, 16 ms requested \`asyncio.sleep\`;
- **slow:** uniform choice among 12, 18, 24 ms requested \`asyncio.sleep\`.

The duration actually observed is a host/event-loop measurement; \`asyncio.sleep\` is not a precise timing guarantee. The regime is disclosed to **both** predictors and calibrated separately. Individual random draws, OS scheduling noise, future service completions, and target recovery timestamps are **not** predictor inputs. No physical link is damaged, no actual network graph is repaired, and the separate monitor polls the service's **own counters**, not independent physical hardware.

The independent monitor process performs read-only \`health\` RPCs approximately every 12 ms, logs both its \`time.monotonic_ns()\` receipt and the server's \`monotonic_ns()\` snapshot, and records \`queued\`, \`active\`, \`accepted\`, \`completed\` and \`available\`. The injector/controller is the parent process and saves timestamped acknowledged job batches and the enable marker in a separate source file. Each original trial, including calibration, uses a fresh server process, fresh monitor process, empty backlog and separate immutable JSONL source records. Source reconstruction must independently verify job IDs, FIFO uniqueness, conservation, source timestamp monotonicity, clock bounds and absence of forbidden arrivals.

## Frozen calibrations and prospective 12-case holdout

Exactly **three calibration trials**, one for each regime in the order fast, standard, slow. For each, enable a fresh worker and submit exactly **18** jobs, with no extra arrivals. The only calibration metric is the median of **13 consecutive server-side completion-to-completion intervals calculated from completed jobs 5 through 18 inclusive** (14 completion timestamps). Each interval must be positive. **No holdout target or source event contributes to calibration.**

Then execute exactly **12 held-out interventions** in this fixed, non-selected order:

| Order | Regime | Jobs queued before enable |
|---:|---|---:|
| 1 | standard | 14 |
| 2 | slow | 10 |
| 3 | fast | 18 |
| 4 | slow | 22 |
| 5 | fast | 10 |
| 6 | standard | 22 |
| 7 | standard | 10 |
| 8 | fast | 22 |
| 9 | slow | 18 |
| 10 | standard | 18 |
| 11 | slow | 14 |
| 12 | fast | 14 |

Each holdout server is initially unavailable. Inject the declared initial jobs, verify all accepted and none completed, then enable. The **same three post-enable arrival batches** are planned for every holdout: exactly 2 more jobs at **+40 ms**, 2 at **+80 ms**, and 2 at **+160 ms** relative to the authenticated server enable timestamp. The injector uses scheduled sleeps; the actual OS acknowledgements are recorded and may deviate from plan. The last-arrival acknowledgement must be **strictly after the prediction landmark**, and the first two batch acknowledgements must be **at or before** the landmark. Any schedule breach marks the entire study invalid; do not reschedule or replace. Total jobs: initial+6. All accepted job IDs must be consecutive, and no job is lost/duplicated.

The **prediction landmark** is the predetermined moment **100 ms after server enable**, using the shared \`time.monotonic_ns()\` domain. The controller must prepare all forecasts no later than **+125 ms** after enable and **strictly before the final +160 ms batch is accepted**. It reads the latest independent-monitor health sample whose **receipt timestamp is ≤ landmark** and within the previous **30 ms**; the sample must be **after enable** and satisfy counter conservation. There must also be a source-validated post-enable monitor sample before that landmark. The source queue state is \`q_m = accepted − completed\` from this single immutable sample. No earlier queue samples or future completed-job information may be fitted into the challenger. The number of accepted arrivals by the landmark is known to both models via timestamped injector acknowledgements and must agree with the monitor sample (an 8–20 ms polling lag must not contain any intervening submissions, guaranteed by +40, +80, +160 ms batches and the at-most-30-ms lookback; disagreement invalidates).

No full trial may be omitted for bad predictions, timing deviations, empty/pre-future queues or slow service. A single missed feature, invalid calibration, stale snapshot, post-enable batch failure, lost monitor role, data integrity contradiction, timeout, or failed original-source digest causes **\`RP_U007_MEASUREMENT_INVALID\`** and no primary claim.

## Exact forecasting formulas and endpoint (frozen before source access)

For each holdout, both predictors know:
- initial accepted workload \`n0\`, the accepted job count through the 100 ms landmark (normally \`n0 + 4\`), the planned last post-enable batch (+160 ms, 2 jobs), the exact 100 ms elapsed since enable, and the **correct regime-specific calibration interval \`s\`** measured in calibration;
- a shared **24 ms** monitoring-latency allowance (two nominal 12 ms monitor periods), used identically in both methods.

Define a *non-fitted source input/capacity baseline*:
\`q_input = max(0, n_accepted_by_landmark − floor(0.100 / s))\`.

Define the *state-observed counterpart*:
\`q_state = q_m\` from exactly one qualifying monitor health sample received no later than landmark.

The fixed **future acknowledged-work count** is 2 (the last +160 ms batch); its actual future acceptance timestamp is unknown at forecast time. The nominal minimum future-arrival lead time is \`0.160 − 0.100 = 0.060 s\`. Both predictions are in seconds elapsed **from the 100 ms landmark**:

\`prediction_input = max((q_input+2)*s, 0.060) + 0.024\`
\`prediction_state = max((q_state+2)*s, 0.060) + 0.024\`

No regressor, fitted coefficients, optimization, extra sensor identity, participant, temporal shift or post-outcome feature is permitted. **Neither model is allowed to use future actual delay, completion events or future monitor samples**. The implementation must write both predictions before last-batch submission and before the target is reached.

The target is \`time_of_second_qualifying_zero_outstanding_monitor_receipt − landmark_time\`, with both qualifying health snapshots **after the actual final post-enable job acceptance**. Two consecutive monitor samples must be separated by at least 8 ms and no more than 120 ms, with no intervening nonzero sample. Count **all outstanding work** (\`accepted−completed = queued+active\`) and verify complete original job conservation. Require at least 3 health snapshots after the final job is accepted and a minimum additional 60 ms of monitor observations after the first qualifying pair. No inferred continuous-time emptiness. Individual source recovery must finish within six seconds; a censored, invalid or missing target cannot be dropped.

**Primary endpoints:** absolute error for \`prediction_input\` and \`prediction_state\` for each of the 12 original trials; their two unrounded mean absolute errors (MAE), and the number of holdouts on which the state predictor has **strictly** smaller error (ties are not wins). All 12 original holdouts are analyzed. The registered success rule is a conjunction:

\`RP_U007_INCREMENTAL_LIVE_STATE_GAIN_WITHIN_HOST\` iff
\`MAE_state <= 0.80 × MAE_input\` **AND** \`state_wins >= 9 of 12\`.

Otherwise, with all sources eligible, report **\`RP_U007_COMPLETE_NO_CONFIRMED_INCREMENT\`**, even if some cases show improvement. This is a predetermined **operational descriptive** threshold, not a calibrated statistical significance test. The order/modes/loads are fixed, not random sample assignments, and no population p-value will be claimed.

Report the **strict zero-added-information control**: reconstruct a simulated \`q_input\` from published input and calibrated interval only, substituting it for the observed state in the second formula. Its prediction must equal \`prediction_input\` **exactly** for all holdouts, proving the experimental comparison is only about measured live state. This is an algorithmic sanity check, not an independent statistical null.

## Scientific limits and broader research question

The experiment evaluates **a single measured FIFO-queue state against an explicitly knowledgeable but imperfect input/capacity estimator**, under disclosed distributions and a single hosted software environment. It does not test a new emergent relation, measure neuronal/cellular persistence, distinguish internal from external faults of a telescope, prove universality, or claim novelty against the queueing literature. A successful result would only mean that, within these software trials, one additional live work-state measurement improved prediction over a calibrated input-aware model according to a fixed threshold. A negative result is a useful calibration finding. A more ambitious next step would require independently instrumented real infrastructure/functional outputs and a much stronger history-aware estimator.
