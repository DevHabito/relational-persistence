# RP-S001-P01 — Structural sufficiency versus disturbance history for persistence

**Registration date:** 8 November 2026. **Base:** unchanged `main` at `214133c5eb33f68cb55f2e10f4b8008097d8d4e8`.  
**Design:** deterministic, exact finite **synthetic** graph reliability study; not ANEEL data, a new law, or a novel algorithm. This protocol must commit **alone** before the primary graph-pair search and outcome computations. Existing RP-F003, RP-F004, RP-U001, RP-U004 results and the literature were already known at registration; this is a new finite operational question, **not independent confirmation of those results**.

## Research question and a conservative falsifier

Can the following deliberately restricted internal-structure summary
`X(G)=(n,m,full labeled degree vector, s-t minimum edge-cut)`,
together with a per-tick edge-deletion **budget** of exactly two, uniquely predict the probability of retaining **a specified terminal-to-terminal path** after random external edge failures? The term "tolerance" here means this declared single Boolean function, not general systemic organization.

We test the **sufficiency claim H_S**: for any two graphs in the frozen finite family with the exact same `X(G)`, the exact one-tick source–target survival fraction under uniformly chosen two-edge deletion is identical. The primary study **rejects H_S** if it finds at least one pair of graphs in the same summary class with different exact rational survival probabilities; otherwise reports no counterexample in this family, **not** proof for arbitrary graphs.

This candidate is intentionally **stronger information** than a simple scalar noise-capacity index because the entire labeled degree vector and terminal cut are matched, but it is still a **coarse summary**; finding a counterexample would not prove the insufficiency of complete topology, a probabilistic reliability polynomial, or a fully identified dynamics model. Static edge cuts and graph reliability are mature prior art.

## Frozen finite family and exact selection

- `V={0,1,2,3,4,5}`, source `s=0`, target `t=5`; undirected simple **labeled** graphs, no loops/multiedges and no edge `(0,5)`.
- Exactly **eight** undirected edges (from the 14 allowed possible edges), graph connected over all six vertices, terminal degrees `deg(0)=deg(5)=2`, and exact minimum `s-t` **edge** cut of size **2**. The s-t mincut is computed by enumerating every nonempty vertex subset containing `0` and excluding `5`, counting crossing edges. No optimization or inference from expected outcomes.
- Enumerate **all** admissible graphs lexicographically as eight-element combinations of sorted possible edges. The theoretical unfiltered edge family has **3,003** combinations, `C(14,8)`. Do not sample, discard hard graphs, or add edge types after seeing results.
- Partition by the **full labeled degree vector**, which strengthens mere sorted-degree matching; all graphs have the same n,m, and frozen terminal cut by construction.
- At each source graph remove exactly **two distinct original edges**, uniformly from its `C(8,2)=28` two-edge subsets. No stochastic Monte Carlo estimates. The original s-t path function survives if and only if a path connects vertices `0` and `5` in the remaining graph. Numerator `K(G)` is exact number of surviving edge-removal subsets, denominator always **28**.
- Pair selection is **the first lexicographic pair of graphs** in a single degree-vector equivalence class with **unequal `K(G)`**, if such pair exists; sorted global lexicographic order of (graph A, graph B), with A < B. In addition report all classes with >=2 graphs, number of qualifying classes, and maximum observed within-class numerator gap, rather than selecting a visually impressive pair or adjusting the family. If no qualifying pair, report `NO_FINITE_COUNTEREXAMPLE`, preserving the negative result.
- Reference oracle A: BFS reachability on the remaining graph; reference oracle B: independently constructed DFS simple-path enumeration on the same remaining graph. An oracle disagreement is `INSTRUMENT_FAILURE`, not a positive result.
- Independent two-edge cut checks: vertex bipartition cut enumeration and deletion of each 1/2-edge subset to test disconnection. All qualifying original graphs must have exactly a two-edge mincut and remain s-t reachable after **every one-edge deletion**.

## Independently specified disturbance *time* control

The **same graph and same one-time edge-failure marginal distribution** (uniform two-edge deletion subsets) can be generated by two competing, explicitly defined processes over exactly **three time observations**. Define `Y_3=1` only if a terminal path survives at **every** one of times 1,2,3; this is sustained survival, not restoration or a claim that time physically emerges.

1. **Frozen failures (maximal temporal persistence):** draw one uniform two-edge failure subset at time 1 and hold that **same** deleted subset for all three observations. Exact survival probability `p_frozen=K/28`.
2. **Renewed independent failures:** at **each** observation draw a fresh independent uniform two-edge deletion subset (all `28^3` ordered sequences equally weighted). Exact sustained survival probability `p_renewed=(K/28)^3`.

The two processes have exactly the same graph, source/target degrees, mincut, two absent edges at each observation, and **same per-time failure distribution**. They differ **only in temporal correlations**. Record both exact rational values for each selected graph. Require `0<K<28` for a strict temporal contrast, and predeclare that if no graph in the admissible family has this property then report `NO_TEMPORAL_CONTRAST`. This is an elementary stochastic-process identity, and earlier RP-F004 studied temporal correlation in scalar sign-noise dynamics; do not claim temporal correlation is a newly discovered principle here.

The “time” horizon of three observations is a fixed **measurement convention**, not a derived physical time variable; no emergent-time inference can follow from this protocol.

## Scientific novelty assessment and explicit nonclaims

These established results **preexist** RP-S001:
- Karl Menger (1927) and the edge-cut/edge-disjoint-path versions through 1956 max-flow/min-cut literature: minimal cut controls *worst-case number of deleted edges needed to disconnect*, not the full conditional probability under a chosen failure distribution.
- Réka Albert, Hawoong Jeong and Albert-László Barabási (2000), “Error and attack tolerance of complex networks,” *Nature* 406, 378–382, DOI 10.1038/35019019: dependence of vulnerability on attack/failure regime.
- Jason I. Brown, Theodore Kolokolnikov and Robert E. Kooij (2024), “New approximations for network reliability,” *Networks* 84(1), 51–63, DOI 10.1002/net.22215: random edge removal, graph reliability and limitations of coarse degree distributions.
- Network robustness and resilience review, *Nature Reviews Physics* 6, 114–131 (2024), DOI 10.1038/s42254-023-00676-y: noise, attack and system-specific dynamics.
- **This same repository's RP-F003 and RP-F004** already demonstrate graph-degree insufficiency for one consensus residual and the importance of noise temporal correlations for a separate stochastic scalar process.

Thus, a verified finite witness here is an **auditably reproduced classroom-scale counterexample within a new declared operational function**, **not a novel general graph theorem or physical discovery**. Finding it does **not** identify a unique scientific law of relational persistence, prove that all structural measures fail, measure real external noise, or demonstrate a real ecological, power-grid, health, software or pregeometric physical process.
