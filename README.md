# Relational Persistence

*When does an organized system remain coherent as information arrives late, imperfectly, or through a limited set of relationships?*

That question is the starting point of this project. We are interested in the conditions under which a networked system preserves or recovers useful coordination while its components are perturbed. We do not begin by assuming that one formula can settle the question for every kind of system.

Relational Persistence is an open, independently reproducible mathematical and computational research program led by **Felipe Gianini Romero**. It grew out of work on the Relational Zero State (RZS) hypothesis and the [Precategorical Modal Field Framework](https://github.com/DevHabito/precategorical-modal-field-framework). These connections motivate questions; they are **not** established deductions, and this repository does not claim a new fundamental law of physics.

## Our first question

If two networks have the same update budget, injected noise variance, and response delay, must they recover from a disturbance at the same rate?

We registered an answerable version of that question **before running the computation**. The protocol fixes two six-node networks, their dynamics, a measurable outcome, and a decision threshold. The outcome is determined by an independently specified transition operator, not generated by the proposed scalar index.

Read the frozen [RP-001 preregistration](docs/preregistration/RP-001.md) before reading the [RP-001 results](docs/results/RP-001.md).

The initial scalar candidate is

\[
C=\frac{B}{I\tau}, \qquad B,I,\tau>0.
\]

We use this only as an **operational index under declared units and proxies**. It is not currently a measured physical persistence variable. In particular, calling it an identity does not make it a prediction. The first protocol tests whether these proxies alone can determine an independently computed dynamical outcome.

## First observed-trajectory benchmark: negative result

Unlike the synthetic dynamics in RP-001, the
[RP-E002-A01 observational benchmark](docs/preregistration/RP-E002-A01.md)
uses *measured traffic speeds* from METR-LA. Its purpose is narrower:
test whether a fixed external sensor graph improves short-horizon
forecasting over ordinary temporal baselines and a permuted-network
control. **It cannot identify the physical index `B/(I*tau)`.**

The initial hosted run [#40043981617](https://github.com/DevHabito/relational-persistence/actions/runs/40043981617)
passed source integrity checks, ran 48 regression tests successfully,
and evaluated 207 sensors over 34,272 recorded timestamps.
**The preregistered alternative failed:** none of the 15-, 30- or
60-minute horizons achieved both of its required 1% improvements.
At 60 minutes, a training-only seasonal baseline substantially
outperformed the lightweight graph model.

Read the complete [negative observational report](docs/results/RP-E002-A01.md),
the [per-horizon reproducibility record](docs/results/RP-E002-A01.json),
and the [source/data policy](data/RP-E002-A01-EXECUTION.md).
This is an informative **negative finding for the specified model**,
not a refutation of graph prediction methods generally or a test
of fundamental physical persistence.

## What is included

- **A falsifiable protocol:** fixed hypotheses, inputs, thresholds, exclusions, and decision rules.
- **Mathematics:** a graph-consensus witness, a spectral derivation, and a separate AR(1) counterexample.
- **Computational audits:** finite-precision checks against an independent matrix-power implementation, negative controls, relabeling invariance, and boundary tests.
- **An observed network case study:** the topology of Zachary's karate club, with simulated dynamics. This is **not** a dataset of observed dynamical persistence.
- **A technical dictionary:** precise definitions, measurement status, units, and claims that are not yet justified.
- **Literature and attribution:** what follows from established mathematics, what is proposed here, and what remains unresolved.
- **A working research ledger:** failed tests and negative findings stay visible.

## Lean 4 formal verification

The registered RP-001 **twenty-step topology contrast** is now supported by
machine-checked real-number inequalities, not only floating-point computations.
For the declared synchronous six-vertex consensus models, the path has a
mean-zero state with twenty-step Euclidean norm ratio strictly above 0.20;
the star has a uniform bound of 0.15 for **every** real mean-zero state.
Consequently, the two worst-case residuals differ by more than the
registered decision threshold of 0.05.

The proof follows the original graph update, with an explicit inverse between
six-coordinate states and `Fin 6 → ℝ`, and an identity relating coordinate
energy to the Euclidean norm. The source is in
[`RealVectorBridge.lean`](RelationalPersistence/RealVectorBridge.lean),
[`RealResidualNorm.lean`](RelationalPersistence/RealResidualNorm.lean),
[`RealGraphBridge.lean`](RelationalPersistence/RealGraphBridge.lean) and
[`RealTwentyStep.lean`](RelationalPersistence/RealTwentyStep.lean).
[Lean 4 verification #40037215631](https://github.com/DevHabito/relational-persistence/actions/runs/40037215631)
succeeded at commit `f681b973a54eb13f7260f52ecf48a228340fc124`.

The original RP-F001 certificate establishes **bounds**, not exact
spectral equalities. Subsequent [RP-F002](https://github.com/DevHabito/relational-persistence/issues/5)
work now also includes two stronger machine-checked classical results:
an explicit path mode attaining the exact ratio
`((8+sqrt(3))/10)^20` ([Lean #40040392019](https://github.com/DevHabito/relational-persistence/actions/runs/40040392019)),
and the exact supremum of achievable nonzero real star ratios,
`(9/10)^20` ([Lean #40041114514](https://github.com/DevHabito/relational-persistence/actions/runs/40041114514)).
The verified source is in [`PathSlowEigenmode.lean`](RelationalPersistence/PathSlowEigenmode.lean)
and [`StarSharpNorm.lean`](RelationalPersistence/StarSharpNorm.lean).

**RP-F002 now has a complete exact twenty-step worst-case result** for the
declared six-vertex path and star, on real mean-zero initial vectors:
`r20(P6)=((8+sqrt(3))/10)^20` and `r20(S6)=(9/10)^20`.
The new [`PathSharpSOS.lean`](RelationalPersistence/PathSharpSOS.lean)
establishes the **universal P6 upper bound** using an explicit sum of

[Lean #40042195359](https://github.com/DevHabito/relational-persistence/actions/runs/40042195359)

These are machine-checked instances of **classical graph consensus and

## Exact equal-degree topology stress test (RP-F003-A01)

The [RP-F003-A01 protocol](docs/preregistration/RP-F003-A01.md) was
committed **before** computing a new graph contrast. Exhaustive, exact
integer arithmetic examined all 32,768 labeled six-vertex simple graphs,
including 26,704 connected graphs. Within the group with identical sorted
degrees `(1,2,2,2,2,3)`, two connected graphs produced normalized
20-step **squared-energy** residuals of about 0.01165 and 0.05373,
a separation of 0.04208 against the predeclared threshold 0.01.

[Results and limits](docs/results/RP-F003-A01.md) include the full
degree-group distribution, exact rational values, independent trace
checks and adversarial tests. A separate [Lean source](RelationalPersistence/DegreeMatchedWitness.lean)
proves a **one-step** matched-degree witness: the pinned
[Lean 4 workflow #40118067207](https://github.com/DevHabito/relational-persistence/actions/runs/40118067207)
passed. This does **not** formally certify the exhaustive 20-step maximum. The distinction between these two
validation scopes remains explicit.

This is a conditional classical consensus counterexample, not
observational validation of the proposed scalar index or any physical law.

## Exact temporal-correlation stress test (RP-F004-A01)

The [RP-F004-A01 preregistration](docs/preregistration/RP-F004-A01.md)
fixes a dimensionless AR(1) state recurrence driven by two symmetric
binary Markov noise processes. Both have the **same one-time noise
distribution**, variance 1/100, gain 1/2 and hypothetical scalar proxies
`B=1, I=1/100, tau=1` (`C=100`). Only sign autocorrelation differs.

The exact limiting output variances are **1/75** and **1/45**.
Their difference **2/225** exceeds the frozen **1/200**
threshold. A 4,096-history full-path exact rational oracle
independently reproduces the 12-step moment recurrence.
[Read the complete result, controls and limitations](docs/results/RP-F004-A01.md)
or inspect the [rational archive](docs/results/RP-F004-A01.json).

[Lean 4 source](RelationalPersistence/ColoredNoiseMoments.lean) now has
[successful formal CI #40120059074](https://github.com/DevHabito/relational-persistence/actions/runs/40120059074)
for rational fixed-point, uniqueness and separation lemmas.
This does **not** formalize a probability space, a stationary stochastic
process, or its limit theorem. Python [CI #40120059073](https://github.com/DevHabito/relational-persistence/actions/runs/40120059073)
passed 148 regression tests on both configured runtimes. This is textbook colored-noise moment algebra and a
conditional scalar-sufficiency counterexample, **not a new physical law**.

## Exact feedback-delay stress test (RP-F005-A01)

The [preregistered RP-F005-A01 protocol](docs/preregistration/RP-F005-A01.md)
fixes the scalar feedback recurrence `x[t+1]=x[t]-k*x[t-d]`
at primary gain `k=1`, comparing actual feedback delays
`d=0` and `d=1`. With `x[0]=x[-1]=1` as appropriate
to each model, the six-step squared residual is exactly
**0 versus 1**, exceeding the predeclared **1/2**
contrast threshold. The delayed gain-one companion
matrix obeys `A³=-Id` and `A⁶=Id`.

The [exact report](docs/results/RP-F005-A01.md) and
[complete rational path archive](docs/results/RP-F005-A01.json)
include 25-step trajectories at two other prespecified gains,
independent matrix-power and scalar-history oracles,
and adversarial tests. [Lean source](RelationalPersistence/DelayedFeedback.lean)
proves the **real six-step identity and finite contraction
obstruction**: [Lean 4 CI #40121843255](https://github.com/DevHabito/relational-persistence/actions/runs/40121843255)
passed on proof head `3982b38`. [Python CI #40121843257](https://github.com/DevHabito/relational-persistence/actions/runs/40121843257)
passed 180 tests on each of Python 3.11 and 3.12.

**The feedback delays are different.** It would be false
to call this a matched-latency test of `B/(I*tau)`.
The result belongs to classical delayed linear control
dynamics, not empirical validation or new physics.

## Scientific boundaries

A classical theorem used here remains a classical theorem. A simulated signal remains simulated. A graph measured in the field does not turn hypothetical dynamics on that graph into empirical validation. A test of a special case is not a proof about nature.

The project will progress through a sequence of auditable claims rather than a sequence of impressive-sounding titles. Results that fail will remain part of the archive, including when they force us to revise the central idea.

See [research scope](docs/foundations/SCOPE.md), [model and proofs](docs/foundations/CONSENSUS_MODEL.md), [dictionary](docs/glossary/DICTIONARY.md), [literature](docs/literature/PRIOR_WORK.md), [research roadmap](docs/protocols/ROADMAP.md), and [status ledger](docs/CLAIM_LEDGER.md).

## RP-F006-A01 — An exact, limited obstruction to queue-state identifiability

Following two registered negative external holdout forecasts,
the [RP-F006-A01 mathematical protocol](docs/preregistration/RP-F006-A01.md)
was frozen on main at `3670d7d` **before the model code or proof**.

A three-job **synthetic** one-server, nonpreemptive
FCFS-compatible experiment constructs two worlds with
**identical complete pre-origin event history, queue snapshots,
historical starts, target requests and running-job age**.
Only the current running job's unobserved actual future
completion differs. A target submitted at zero then has
waiting time **1 or 100 seconds** respectively.

For any deterministic predictor using solely the same
observation, the two-world maximum absolute prediction error
is at least **99/2=49.5 seconds**. Predicting **101/2=50.5**
achieves this bound. This is the elementary classical
triangle-inequality obstruction; it is **not a new
queueing theorem**, a real HPC performance estimate, or a
claim that no statistical predictor is possible.

The first original [synthetic GitHub Actions certificate #40205665104](https://github.com/DevHabito/relational-persistence/actions/runs/40205665104)
returned `FINITE_NONIDENTIFIABILITY_VERIFIED`, archived
unaltered at `caab6ad`. The
[Lean 4 proof](RelationalPersistence/QueueObservability.lean)
passed at [run #40205365501](https://github.com/DevHabito/relational-persistence/actions/runs/40205365501)
with 499 Python regressions per supported version.
The source model **does not assert universal work conservation**:
its earlier jobs have fixed one-second dispatch delays.
Read the [complete report](docs/results/RP-F006-A01.md).

This supplies a formal *possible missing-information
mechanism*, **not evidence that it caused RP-E010 or
RP-E011's negative external outcomes**. Neither `C=B/(I*tau)`
nor a physical persistence law was tested.

## RP-F007-A01 — What a GUARANTEED residual-service bound can tell us

The separate [RP-F007-A01 protocol](docs/preregistration/RP-F007-A01.md)
was frozen at `93d5dc4` before any new code, proof or
synthetic calculation, building on RP-F006's known finite
nonidentifiability result. An **enforced**, public running
job walltime cap changes the set of possible future waits
when its elapsed execution time is known.

Under the declared synthetic one-server FCFS-compatible
model, a job already running for 30 seconds and
subject to **hard cap 500 seconds** permits all 470
integer future waits 1..470. The exact minimax
absolute-error prediction is **235.5 s**, with
**234.5 s worst-case error**. Under an actually
enforced **90-second cap**, all 60 waits 1..60
are admissible, midpoint **30.5 s** and sharp
worst-case error **29.5 s**.

The two groups have DIFFERENT known public caps,
so their input information is NOT equal between
groups. The 205-second difference in synthetic
minimax worst-case error is **not empirical
forecasting improvement, a causal scheduler
effect or a new queuing theorem**.

The original first hosted
[RP-F007-A01 run #40229910510](https://github.com/DevHabito/relational-persistence/actions/runs/40229910510)
verified all **530** preregistered synthetic
scenarios and adversarial controls, archived
unaltered at `6d0d794`. The
[Lean 4 proof](RelationalPersistence/ResidualBounds.lean)
was approved before the code merged at `4023bb1`.
A **requested** walltime is not necessarily
an ENFORCED cap; a 501-second actual runtime
under a nominal 500-second limit was the
predeclared adverse control. No external
E010/E011 holdout was reused, and the empirical
negative findings remain negative.

See the [full F007 report](docs/results/RP-F007-A01.md).
No universal physical relational persistence law
or scalar `C=B/(I*tau)` was verified.
