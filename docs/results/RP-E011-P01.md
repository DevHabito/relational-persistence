# RP-E011-P01 — Original external queue-aware forecast: negative result

**Permanent first-run verdict: `NO_REGISTERED_QUEUE_GAIN`.**

This was a valid, preregistered observational prediction comparison on
independently collected historical workload data. The candidate two-sample
per-queue trend **lost** to both of its primary classical controls
on the fixed 169,189-job chronological holdout. The loss is a scientific
result, not a source-access failure, not grounds to replace the holdout,
and not a proof or disproof of any universal physical law.

## Fixed target and historical features

The target was SWF submission-to-start waiting time in **seconds**,
not the earlier service-recovery time. The original 22-field CERIT-SC
records were parsed under the frozen D04 source conditions,
excluding precisely the already-known first-line ID 0. All **169,189**
eligible future-period job waits, including extremes, were scored.
There were **no zero-wait** jobs observed in this heldout population;
that is a reported source property, not a removal rule.

At each submission time `t`, queue waiting counts were independently
reconstructed from events with timestamp **strictly less** than
`t-120` and `t-60`. Only actual starts strictly prior to
a relevant cutoff contributed historical waiting-time labels.
The candidate was the nonnegative extrapolation
`max(0, 2*q_last - q_prev)` divided by the previously observed
per-queue start rate. The two primary controls were (a) the
unextrapolated `q_last` divided by that same past rate and (b)
the recently started-job wait median in the same queue, favoring
the requested-processor-size group if at least 16 previous starts
were available. All details, fallbacks and 64-job history cap
were fixed **before** original predictions.

Two independently implemented queue-state algorithms (sorted
binary-event counting and chronological event sweep) agreed on
all **212,964 distinct queried queue/snapshot pairs**. There were
**8,473** heldout targets with no recorded per-queue starts
in the frozen historical rate interval; as predeclared, their
STATIC and TREND predictions both fell back to the same historical
median, and those jobs **remained included** in the scores.
The evaluation was sequential/online: a prior heldout job could
enter a *later* history only after its actual start was observed.
No test job's own future wait was used in its forecast.

## Primary result — unweighted, untrimmed MAE

| Registered model | Heldout MAE (seconds) | Role |
|---|---:|---|
| Recently started queue/resource-group wait median | **34,309.4318** | PRIMARY classical historical comparator |
| Static per-queue backlog / prior start rate | **43,953.9763** | PRIMARY classical state comparator |
| Two-snapshot per-queue trend / prior start rate | **45,328.0125** | Frozen candidate; FAILED |

The trend MAE was approximately **3.13% higher than STATIC**
and **32.12% higher than HISTORY**. The registered PASS required
its MAE to be **at least 10% LOWER than each** primary control.
This conjunction failed, despite valid source and causal-oracle
checks. No subgroup selection or ex-post parameter change is
allowed to replace the original `NO_REGISTERED_QUEUE_GAIN`.

The original first-run full-precision MAEs and biases
are in [`audit.json`](RP-E011-P01-first-run/audit.json).

## Coverage caveat: most heldout jobs came from two queues

Although the original file had **13 distinct real queue labels**,
the heldout period was **strongly imbalanced**:

- The largest queue contributed **149,760** heldout jobs.
- The second-largest contributed **18,880**.
- Those two sum to **168,640 / 169,189**, or **99.68%** of all
  scored targets. Only **549** jobs came from the other queues.
- **Seven** of the original 13 queues had **zero** heldout
  targets; all remain explicitly represented with a zero count
  and undefined per-queue MAE, not silently dropped.

The other four nonempty heldout queues had **469**, **67**,
**12** and **1** job. Per-queue MAE is descriptive and
especially unstable for the tiny subgroups. The preregistered
aggregate performance mainly describes the two dominant
queues, not generality across all 13 queues or deployments.

full-holdout endpoint. Small and large groups favored
the historical control.

## Claim boundaries and implications

This independent observational source confirms that the particular
**two-snapshot per-queue extrapolation** did NOT add the
preregistered forecasting value once realistic classical
historical and static queue controls were included. It
reinforces the need to avoid inferring transfer from positive
RP-E007/RP-E009 controlled software runs. The historical HPC2N
RP-E010 negative is an additional, different-workload result,
not an identical replication: source curation, service topology,
targets, and modeling controls differ.

No random allocation, queue-policy intervention or physical
recovery measurement took place. Submission-to-start wait
is subject to changing multi-resource scheduling and shared
virtualized infrastructure. Jobs share clusters and events,
so 169,189 target rows are **not 169,189 independent physical
experiments**. The empirical negative says nothing decisive
about the universal existence or nonexistence of a relational
persistence scalar `C=B/(I*tau)`, which was not measured
or fitted. No new Lean theorem is claimed.

Further research needs a separate, independently justified
question: for example, preregistered out-of-source
transfer to a *new* cluster/year or a formally identified
additional causal state. Do not re-optimize on this already
opened holdout and present it as unseen validation.
The original unfavorable result must remain first-class evidence.
