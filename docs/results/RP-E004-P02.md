# RP-E004-P02 — fully sampled software instrumentation pilot

**Primary verdict: PASS_AUDITABLE_INSTRUMENTATION.**

This is a second, separately preregistered single-host **software
measurement-feasibility** study. The project has **not** validated
`C=B/(I*tau)` as a predictor, physical mechanism, or universal law.

## Actual run and outcomes

Order was fixed by seed `20261024` and realized as:
`sham-01`, `positive-controlled-01`,
`positive-disabled-01`, `positive-controlled-02`.

| Trial | Calibrated B (work/s) | Injected I (work/s) | Service-applied tau | Sampled Y | Candidate C (1/s) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Positive, controlled 01 | 82.22617 | 254.47973 | 81.660 ms | 342.986 ms | 3.95682 |
| Positive, controlled 02 | 82.26491 | 253.38385 | 82.178 ms | 327.449 ms | 3.95075 |
| Positive, controller disabled | 82.26388 | Actual positive load | No controller action | 1.029 s | **Undefined** |
| Sham | 82.26380 | 0 | No controller action | Not a positive-dose outcome | **Undefined** |

Both controlled positive trials and the controller-disabled positive
control delivered exactly **120 accepted HTTP tasks** each; sham
delivered **zero**. The controller's actual service-applied timestamps
are recorded by the service under its update lock, separately from
client send and acknowledgement times.

**Timing honesty:** the reported tau is service-side change
timestamp minus externally logged injector onset, not programmed
delay or client acknowledgement. The measured client request-to-ack
durations were approximately **1.013 ms** and **0.690 ms** in the
controlled positive trials, respectively.

Both controlled positive monitors archived all samples and observed
recovery with the specified 500-ms sampled dwell. The disabled
controller also recovered, but more slowly, which is compatible
with ordinary FIFO draining at the unmodified service rate; it
**does not** establish a statistical causal relation to C. It
was one controller-disabled control, not an independently randomized
multi-run causal trial.

### Sampling and replay

| Trial | Full monitor samples | Samples after injection end | Maximum post-end gap |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Controlled 01 | 59 | 34 | 26.532 ms |
| Controlled 02 | 57 | 33 | 26.820 ms |
| Controller disabled | 83 | 60 | 26.763 ms |
| Sham | 42 | 21 | 26.521 ms |

Every maximum interval was below the preregistered **150-ms** cutoff.
The per-sample JSONL data includes client probe time, server-side
snapshot time, queue size, active workers and completion count.
The replay tests verify the recovery timestamp from the retained
samples and the *separately* retained end-of-injection marker.
No interpolation is used. In particular, a deleted block of
monitor samples must fail if it creates a gap above the frozen bound.

All three pretrial saturation windows in each trial had a positive
queue depth at their sampled boundaries and positive completions.
A separate twelve-request sparse probe served as the contrasting
workload, not a substitute capacity measure. Per-window rate
variation remained within the preregistered 25% tolerance.

## Explicit limits and scientific interpretation

1. **Sampling only:** a successful replay proves the software
   recovered according to **sampled** queue/worker observations.
   It does **not** prove that idle state persisted continuously
   at every unobserved instant.
2. **One host:** controller, injector, service and monitor are
   separate processes **sharing one OS/kernel/clock and resources**.
   Cross-machine synchronization, hardware instrument
   independence and external replication remain unverified.
3. **Programmed mechanism:** the controller deliberately changes
   worker sleep from 12 to 6 ms when observed backlog is above
   five tasks. Faster queue drainage is predicted by classical
   work-conservation/queueing considerations; the positive
   outcome is not a newly discovered law.
4. **Two enabled positive interventions:** these and a single
   disabled positive control cannot provide reliable
   statistical power, isolate run-order effects or generalize
   to other systems. The disabled control was not preregistered
   as a powered between-treatment causal analysis.
5. **B and I are rates:** `B/(I*tau)` therefore has units
   **1/s**, and is undefined when positive intensity or
   the actual action timestamp is absent. It cannot be
   compared without qualification to the earlier variance-like
   RP-001 index.
6. **No hypothesis test:** neither the quotient nor any
   fitted combination of its components was evaluated
   against held-out recovery outcomes here. No claim for
   or against its predictive adequacy can follow.

**Conclusion:** RP-E004-P02 fixed the P01's principal *trace
auditability* deficiency and showed how to distinguish internal
service action from client acknowledgement in a bounded
single-host test service. The preregistered instrumentation-quality
gate passed. A future test of physical persistence must be a
new protocol, with calibrated instruments, independent systems,
a competing classical baseline, adequate variation and
a genuinely held-out recovery prediction.
