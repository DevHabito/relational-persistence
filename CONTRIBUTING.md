# Contributing to Relational Persistence

Challenge the assumptions and the mathematics before trying to confirm an attractive conclusion. Small reproducible counterexamples are welcome. So are clear negative results.

For each proposed claim, provide its precise scope, definitions, units, provenance, falsification conditions, proof or algorithm, and known counterexamples. For every measurement, distinguish a public observed variable from a simulated or inferred one. Include tests and a failure mode. Keep software and documentation in English, with descriptive names and without unsupported claims of novelty.

Do not edit a registered hypothesis to match its own outcome. New measurements or assumptions require a new protocol ID; link corrections to their original record. Do not erase failed experiments or reuse test observations as training data without declaring that choice.

Issues should make the underlying scientific question understandable without requiring trust in the author. Ethical use of data and license compliance are required. Avoid publishing private observations or identifiable experimental participants.
