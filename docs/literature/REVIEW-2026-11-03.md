## Graph paths, cuts and restoration

**Karl Menger (1927), "Zur allgemeinen Kurventheorie."** Early path–separator result, classically known as Menger's theorem. A historical treatment at the Centrum Wiskunde & Informatica explains the original title and the graph-theoretic interpretation: the maximum number of vertex-disjoint routes is connected to minimum separating sets. [Historical source (CWI)](https://homepages.cwi.nl/~lex/files/histco.pdf). I use Menger's theorem as **prior mathematics** behind the difference between one surviving route (F1) and two internally vertex-disjoint routes (F2). A specific finite evaluation in Lean is not a new theorem about vertex connectivity.

**Fuyu Hu, Chi Ho Yeung, Saini Yang, Weiping Wang and An Zeng (14 April 2016), "Recovery of infrastructure networks after localised attacks."** *Scientific Reports* 6, article 24522. DOI: [10.1038/srep24522](https://doi.org/10.1038/srep24522). [Publisher record](https://www.nature.com/articles/srep24522). The paper compares deliberate recovery strategies after localized disruptions and includes a greedy restoration strategy. It precedes RP-U003's path-aware repair scheduling; the present benchmark is not the discovery of informed graph restoration.

**Igor Averbakh and Jordi Pereira (21 September 2018 online), "Lateness Minimization in Pairwise Connectivity Restoration Problems."** *INFORMS Journal on Computing* 30(3), 522–538. DOI: [10.1287/ijoc.2017.0796](https://doi.org/10.1287/ijoc.2017.0796). [Publisher record](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/ijoc.2017.0796). The work addresses construction and repair schedules, pairs that regain connectivity and explicit optimization criteria. It is a relevant preexisting baseline for any proposal to optimize functional reconnection over time.

**Reza Olfati-Saber, J. Alex Fax and Richard M. Murray (January 2007), "Consensus and Cooperation in Networked Multi-Agent Systems."** *Proceedings of the IEEE* 95(1), 215–233. DOI: [10.1109/JPROC.2006.887293](https://doi.org/10.1109/JPROC.2006.887293). [Author's publication page](https://murray.cds.caltech.edu/index.php/Consensus_and_Cooperation_in_Networked_Multi-Agent_Systems). This is prior work for graph spectra, consensus dynamics, communication losses and delay. My finite Laplacian and feedback examples do not originate this theory.

## Resilience in complex networks and ecology

**C. S. Holling (1 November 1973), "Resilience and Stability of Ecological Systems."** *Annual Review of Ecology and Systematics* 4, 1–23. DOI: [10.1146/annurev.es.04.110173.000245](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev.es.04.110173.000245). [Publisher record](https://www.annualreviews.org/content/journals/10.1146/annurev.es.04.110173.000245). Ecological resilience predates this project. A six-node graph with an artificial component replacement is not an ecological field study.

**Eric Desjardins, Gillian Barker, Zoë Lindo, Catherine Dieleman and Antoine C. Dussault (June 2015), "Promoting Resilience."** *The Quarterly Review of Biology* 90(2), 147–165. DOI: [10.1086/681439](https://doi.org/10.1086/681439). [PubMed bibliography](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26285353/). The paper discusses structural and response diversity, functional redundancy and the many meanings of ecological resilience. These ideas are not introduced by calling a connectivity criterion "functional continuity."

**Jianxi Gao, Baruch Barzel and Albert-László Barabási (17 February 2016), "Universal resilience patterns in complex networks."** *Nature* 530, 307–312. DOI: [10.1038/nature16948](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature16948). [Publisher article](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature16948). The same authors published an [erratum on 4 May 2016](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature18019) and an [author correction on 28 March 2019](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-019-1091-9). Their framework and its corrections need to be considered in any future universality claim. A finite graph contrast alone does not establish or overturn a network-universality theorem.

## Queueing, operational data and external validation

**John D. C. Little (1 June 1961 online), "A Proof for the Queuing Formula: L = λW."** *Operations Research* 9(3), 383–387. DOI: [10.1287/opre.9.3.383](https://doi.org/10.1287/opre.9.3.383). [Publisher record](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/abs/10.1287/opre.9.3.383). Classical queueing relationships depend on their stated stochastic and time-average assumptions. The project's ad hoc backlog/start-rate baselines must not be identified with Little's law without proving its conditions.

**Dror G. Feitelson, Dan Tsafrir and David Krakov (October 2014), "Experience with using the Parallel Workloads Archive."** *Journal of Parallel and Distributed Computing* 74(10), 2967–2982. DOI: [10.1016/j.jpdc.2014.06.013](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jpdc.2014.06.013). [Publisher record](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0743731514001154). The authors discuss real workload-log quality and provenance problems. This is directly relevant to the failed and source-blocked external queue forecasts; it does not rescue a rejected source by retroactively changing its exclusion rules.

## The boundary between earlier scholarship and this repository

The literature already includes vertex connectivity and graph cuts (Menger), graph restoration and optimization (Hu; Averbakh and Pereira), consensus and networked control (Olfati-Saber, Fax and Murray), ecological resilience and functional redundancy (Holling; Desjardins and colleagues), complex-network resilience with explicit corrections (Gao, Barzel and Barabási), and queueing/workload methodology (Little; Feitelson and colleagues).

The work recorded in this repository is narrower: **frozen model definitions, finite counterexamples, exact constrained graph enumeration, selected Lean 4 certificates, first-run-preserved software measurements and candid external prediction failures**. The dated [research record](../research/RESEARCH-RECORD-2026-11-03.md) links each category to its own protocol or source report.

If a future result appears new, I will formulate the exact statement and compare it with earlier theorems and experiments **before** making a priority claim. I still need a full literature search in network reliability, temporal graphs, stochastic systems, control theory, resilience metrics and application-specific engineering. A DOI verifies a bibliographic identity, not the truth of my proposed mechanism.
