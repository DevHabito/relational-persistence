# RP-U004-P01 — Degree-matched finite repair policies with shared information

**Study type:** prospective, synthetic, deterministic graph-restoration comparison.  
**Protocol date:** 3 November 2026.  
**Research lead:** Felipe Gianini Romero.

## Why I am running this experiment

RP-U003-A01 used equal numbers of rebuilt edges and found an F1 tie between GREEDY1 and LOOKAHEAD2, even though lookahead improved the stronger F2 metric. It did **not** equalize information access, CPU use, or provide independent natural-system evidence. I want to see whether a two-choice lookahead rule still offers any benefit against an F2-aware greedy method when the algorithms have access to the **same complete precomputed function table**. I will not interpret a win against an uninformative label-order or degree-only baseline as a discovery.

This is established graph connectivity and restoration optimization, not new theory. Prior work includes:

- **Fuyu Hu and colleagues (2016)**, *Recovery of infrastructure networks after localised attacks*, *Scientific Reports* 6:24522, https://doi.org/10.1038/srep24522. Greedy and other repair strategies were already compared.
- **Novella Bartolini, Stefano Ciavarella, Thomas F. La Porta and Simone Silvestri (2017)**, *On Critical Service Recovery After Massive Network Failures*, *IEEE/ACM Transactions on Networking*, https://doi.org/10.1109/TNET.2017.2688330. Critical-service restoration and optimization are established problems.
- **Igor Averbakh and Jordi Pereira (2018)**, *Lateness Minimization in Pairwise Connectivity Restoration Problems*, *INFORMS Journal on Computing*, https://doi.org/10.1287/ijoc.2017.0796.
- **Fenghua Wang, Hale Cetinay, Zhidong He, Le Liu, Piet Van Mieghem and Robert E. Kooij (17 October 2023)**, *Recovering Power Grids Using Strategies Based on Network Metrics and Greedy Algorithms*, *Entropy* 25(10):1455, https://doi.org/10.3390/e25101455. One-step and two-step greedy recovery were already investigated in electricity networks with a DC power-flow model.
- **Abigail J. Crocker and Stephen D. Boyles (23 April 2025)**, *Heuristic Selection in Disaster Recovery Sequencing*, *Journal of Infrastructure Systems* 31(3), https://doi.org/10.1061/JITSE4.ISENG-2467. Existing repair sequencing algorithms, including beam search and simulated annealing, were compared.

These sources justify stronger baselines. They do not establish my particular finite scores, which I have not computed.

## Fixed graph family before outcome inspection

Let `V = {0,1,2,3,4,5,6}`, source `s=0`, target `t=6`, and removable internal nodes `U={1,2,3,4,5}`. Graphs are simple, undirected, unlweighted and **labeled**. Exactly eight original edges are present, the direct terminal edge `(0,6)` is excluded, and source and target each have degree exactly two. All seven original vertices must be connected.

There are 20 eligible unordered edges excluding `(0,6)`. Independent enumeration A visits `itertools.combinations` of these 20 edges in groups of eight and filters degrees and connectivity using BFS. Independent enumeration B chooses exactly two incident edges from `0` to distinct internal nodes, two incident edges from `6` to distinct internal nodes, and four further edges among the ten internal unordered pairs; it checks connectivity using a separately coded DFS. Both must produce the **same lexicographically sorted set** of accepted labeled graphs. The unfiltered B construction has `C(5,2)^2 C(10,4)=21,000` candidates, a combinatorial planning identity rather than an observed acceptance count. Do not set the accepted-graph count or a family SHA from a future run.

Group all accepted graphs by the **full labeled degree tuple** `(deg(0),...,deg(6))`, not merely by terminal degree or sorted multiset. Retain every degree stratum containing at least two distinct graphs; from each such stratum select the **lexicographically first and lexicographically last** edge tuple, both. Strata with only one graph are recorded and excluded from the pair comparison. This deterministic structural rule is frozen before computing any connectivity-repair outcome. No graph is added, excluded, randomized, resampled or relabeled after looking at a score. Distinct labelings or paired graphs are **not independent population draws**, and min/max selection can itself induce a structural bias.

Each selected graph is independently reset for removal of **every** internal node `v∈{1,...,5}`. We will report the number of accepted graphs, full-degree strata, selected matched pairs, scenarios, original degree patterns and the complete lex-ordered selected-family SHA-256. Reject empty sets, duplicated graphs, broken pairs or degree mismatches.

## Fixed repair timeline and identical action costs

At `t=0`, the original graph is intact. At `t=1`, remove one chosen original internal vertex `v` and all incident edges. At `t=2`, insert a different replacement identity `7+v`. Rebuild exactly one edge between that identity and one previously adjacent surviving vertex on each tick `t=2,3,4,5,6` while any unrepaired eligible neighbor remains; otherwise leave the state unchanged. The old vertex never returns. The original topology and all possible old neighbors are public knowledge in this **toy** problem. There are no new failures, no adaptive graph rewiring beyond the allowed edges, no resource capacities, no actual clock units, and no biologically meaningful restoration.

All strategies must have precisely the same present-vertex count, edge count and total number of repaired edges at every scenario/tick. One repaired edge has one **model cost unit**, not one dollar or a measured unit of labor. The common primary time horizon is `t=1..6` inclusive (six post-failure model ticks).

## Operational functions and independent oracles

`F1(S)` is Boolean reachability from source to target by at least one undirected simple path. `F2(S)` is Boolean existence of at least two internally vertex-disjoint source-to-target simple paths. `F2 ⇒ F1` must always hold. The direct terminal link is forbidden.

For every unique repaired neighbor subset, precompute an immutable table of `(F1,F2)` indexed by the canonical subset. Compute F1 independently using BFS and exhaustive simple-path DFS; compute F2 independently with a pair of interior-disjoint simple paths and by checking that deletion of **each** present internal vertex preserves source-to-target reachability. Every pair must agree exactly. If one disagrees, the study is **INVALID**, not a model failure.

All strategies may access the **same** complete original graph, the same known eligible repair neighbors, the same task and the same precomputed `(F1,F2)` subset table. This equalizes **available model information** and upfront oracle-table construction, not decision-time CPU cost. Report exact lookup/comparison work per strategy separately; do not claim equal execution time.

## Six fixed decision strategies

Choose one unused eligible neighbor at each repair tick, never repeat one, and break every final tie by **ascending neighbor label**. Freeze these decision rules:

1. **ASC:** select the numerically smallest unused neighbor.
2. **DEGREE:** select highest **original pre-failure** degree, then lowest label.
3. **GREEDY_F1:** maximize immediate lexicographic `(F1,F2)`, then lowest label.
4. **GREEDY_F2:** maximize immediate lexicographic `(F2,F1)`, then lowest label.
5. **LOOKAHEAD2_F1:** for each first candidate `a`, obtain the **best** pair `(F1,F2)` available after `a` and one distinct future remaining neighbor, then rank `a` by `(best_future_F1,best_future_F2,immediate_F1,immediate_F2)`, then lowest label.
6. **LOOKAHEAD2_F2:** same two-choice evaluation, but rank `a` by `(best_future_F2,best_future_F1,immediate_F2,immediate_F1)`, then lowest label.

For the last available neighbor, future merit equals immediate merit. A strategy is deterministic and re-evaluated after each chosen repair, using only the shared static synthetic table. Two-step methods are not granted a future measured label or unseen event.

Separately enumerate **all permutations of the originally eligible repair neighbors** for each scenario and calculate the maximum integrated F1 and maximum integrated F2, with their different optimal orders disclosed. These are **offline, full-horizon upper bounds** for the declared toy task, not deployable comparator predictions or a single simultaneously achievable optimum. Every strategy's cumulative F1/F2 must be at most its corresponding bound. Compare strategies using only pre-registered original functions, not a post-hoc composite score.

## Frozen questions and outcome gates

**Primary H1 (directional):** `LOOKAHEAD2_F2` must achieve a **strictly larger** aggregate number of successful F2 case-ticks than `GREEDY_F2` over all selected matched-degree graphs, all five original internal-node losses and all six post-failure ticks. Any tie or loss declares `REGISTERED_F2_LOOKAHEAD_ADVANTAGE_NOT_SUPPORTED`. A win declares `FINITE_MATCHED_DEGREE_F2_LOOKAHEAD_ADVANTAGE_SUPPORTED`. Both decisions remain finite synthetic findings only.

**Secondary H2 (independent directional):** test whether `LOOKAHEAD2_F1` strictly exceeds `GREEDY_F1` in aggregate F1. This test may fail even if H1 succeeds. Report the actual inequality without making it a retrospective primary claim.

Additional required analyses, regardless of outcome:

- Exact F1/F2 integer totals for **all six policies**, with numerator and common denominator, no fabricated probabilities or confidence intervals.
- Exact per-scenario wins, ties and losses for both lookahead-versus-matched-greedy comparisons and cases in which lookahead is worse.
- Original F2 presence; degree tuple and paired-graph stratum outcomes, including adverse strata and near-equal matched-degree pairs.
- Integrated F1/F2 regret to the independent per-scenario full-horizon permutation maxima.
- Total model-truth-table entries, exact strategy lookup/comparison counts, and **descriptive** CPU timing if measured. Never call equal edge cost equal CPU cost.
- Relabeling sensitivity under a fixed cyclic internal mapping `1→2→3→4→5→1` (terminals fixed) on selected cases: report changed strategy function outcomes, not selectively omit them. Labeled tie rules can change behavior.
- Full case-level original edge tuples, failed vertex, policy repair sequence, all six post-failure F1/F2 states, identical cost trajectory, offline bounds, and stable SHA-256 over the serialized case trace.

The micro-average over labeled scenarios is an exact census of the **selected finite family**, not an estimate for independent random networks or future field reliability.

## Boundaries and next question

Even a clean H1 pass is not an original graph theorem, a new physical law, a Lean proof of the whole Python program, or field evidence for ecological recovery, healthcare quality, aerospace reliability, power-grid stability or `C=B/(I*tau)`. The comparison still uses a precomputed function oracle and ideal blueprint information. A negative result is scientifically useful and stays in the permanent archive.

Only after this synthetic study may I register a separate external test whose target is a real operational service and whose graph, historical inputs, costs and control actions are independently measurable. No previously inspected RP-E010/E011 forecast holdout can be reused as a blind test.
