## The problem, in its actual historical order

The original D07 source audit found **zero eligible candidate-key rows**. That was a negative result of its declared *two-character* month format, not evidence of zero real incident relations. The independent D08 follow-up documented that the actual source month fields were **un-padded** strings `1`–`8` in the occurrence file and `1`–`7` in the interruption file. D08 also established that **1,929,271 occurrence records** and **1,293,508 interruption records** had codes *longer than the ten characters declared in ANEEL's 2026 dictionary*, without recording any individual codes.

These findings were already known before D09 was registered. D09 is therefore **retrospective** in research development, although its two parallel counting procedures and their stop rules were written down before this first D09 source-key query. No original D07 failure or D08 first-run evidence has been changed or represented as a new unseen prediction.

D09 asked how many unmodified literal **text keys** appear in both ANEEL 2026 sources if we preserve the actual unpadded months but do not silently decide whether longer original codes should be excluded or included. The objective was to measure **both** alternatives, not to select the one with more matches.

## Registered key, variants and source eligibility

Each candidate key concatenates **unchanged** (1) 14-character ASCII-digit distributor CNPJ, (2) literal competence year `2026`, (3) exact unpadded month `1`–`12`, and (4) the exact literal occurrence code from the two dictionaries' differently named fields. No integer casting, ID truncation, leading-zero removal, case change, approximate matching, time harmonization or undocumented crosswalk was applied.

We ran both variants over the **same complete original files**:

- **Cohort S, documented width:** admit original occurrence code strings of length **1–10** only, discarding longer codes *from the eligible candidate-key population*, **not** from the original source row count.
- **Cohort F, original full code:** admit complete original source strings within the pre-existing 1 MiB CSV field limit, including longer-than-ten-character codes *without asserting that the publisher dictionary's width is semantically wrong or obsolete*.

Both have explicit ASCII, nonempty, no surrounding whitespace, year/month and distributor validation. The F population contains every source row in the two exact files. The S population contains 4,860,213 occurrence rows and 4,784,823 interruption rows. The excluded S rows (**1,929,271** and **1,293,508**) are precisely the long-code rows already documented by D08; no other full-key field failed the D09 syntax predicates. Both total original source denominators remain unchanged.

| Exactly one occurrence row and multiple interruption rows | 252,093 | 301,957 |

| **Total** | **2,968,556** | **3,609,614** |

The occurrence file has only **483 repeated candidate keys** under either cohort. All 483 are observed exactly twice (**966 source rows**). By contrast, the interruption file contains **271,970 repeated distinct keys** in cohort S and **366,157** in cohort F, accounting for **1,874,122** and **2,288,824** source rows respectively. Keeping source rows separate from distinct keys was therefore essential.

The one-to-many label is purely a statement about **the multiplicity of exact publisher text fields in these two files**, not a proven causal ordering or claim that one crew repaired multiple physical failures. Even the 1:1-key category does not certify that two publisher records refer to the same real-world event.

### What the long codes contribute, without choosing a winning rule

The full-code cohort F contains **641,058 additional *matching distinct textual keys*** whose code is longer than ten characters. These account for **641,058 occurrence source rows** and **787,884 interruption source rows** with matching full long-code keys. They are *not* matched by truncating or coercing a long string into a short one.

Of the original F-eligible long-code population, **1,288,213** occurrence rows and **505,624** interruption rows remain unmatched under the complete four-field text key. Distinct unmatched long-code keys are **1,288,213** and **331,935**, respectively.

The same short-code subset, including its matched keys and source rows, was independently derived from F and from S and matched exactly. The S/F identities, four multiplicity group sums, each source's original/eligible/invalid rows and exact full-byte fingerprints all passed. No extra correspondence was manufactured by rounding, dropping duplicated rows or redefining the key after reading the results.

There is still an unresolved **dictionary/data disagreement** concerning the stated 10-character field. The longer codes are real *source strings* in this exact SHA-frozen publication; this audit cannot decide whether they are valid physical occurrence references, distinct namespaces, changes in publication conventions or a documentation problem. This remains an external semantic validation question.

## Existing scientific method and original contributions

Exact and probabilistic record linkage long predate this research. In **Ivan P. Fellegi and Alan B. Sunter (1969),** “A Theory for Record Linkage,” *Journal of the American Statistical Association*, 64(328), pp. 1183–1210, DOI [10.1080/01621459.1969.10501049](https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.1969.10501049), the central task is to decide whether records refer to the same entity or if available evidence remains insufficient. **Peter Christen (2012),** *Data Matching: Concepts and Techniques for Record Linkage, Entity Resolution, and Duplicate Detection*, Springer, DOI [10.1007/978-3-642-31164-2](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-642-31164-2), explicitly discusses preprocessing, indexing, comparison, evaluation of matching quality, complexity and privacy. Both were checked against the publisher's bibliographic records during this study.

D09's deterministic equality grouping and SQL conservation checks are **not a new record linkage theory**. This project contributes only a reproducible, source-version-pinned description of exactly how *these two ANEEL 2026 published files* relate under two disclosed code-width conventions, including first-run failures and limitations. Source publishers: [ANEEL emergency occurrences](https://dadosabertos.aneel.gov.br/dataset/ocorrencias-emergenciais-nas-redes-de-distribuicao), [ANEEL interruptions](https://dadosabertos.aneel.gov.br/dataset/interrupcoes-de-energia-eletrica-nas-redes-de-distribuicao).

## Not established and next source gate

Even millions of exact matching keys do **not** establish:

- real-world event identity or an error-rated match classifier;
- temporal overlap, compatible event start/end, consistent feeder/substation codes, independent event outcomes or causally available pre-decision information;
- whether published 2026 resources cover all companies, all dates or all electric incidents;
- historical switching configurations, repair crew operations, actual choice of feasible edges, true CPU or field repair costs;
- a physically correct F2 criterion for usually radially energized distribution feeders, a greedy/lookahead advantage, novel Lean mathematics or a universal relational-persistence law.

A separate D10 **temporal and operational consistency check** could now register its candidate comparison groups and expected timing/feeder rules before inspecting any matched original date or network metadata. It must preserve every contradicting or ambiguous pair in the denominators and must not infer a physical repair action from an event label. If original input histories are incomplete, that is a legitimate negative result.

**D09 conclusion:** the input files contain reproducible, substantial **literal-key correspondence** and many interruption records sharing the same candidate key. The observed result is real **as an equality relation over two fixed CSV files**. Its interpretation as a joint, physical power-grid restoration population remains unverified.
