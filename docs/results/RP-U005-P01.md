# RP-U005-P01 — A repaired network can retain unrecovered service backlog

**17 November 2026.** Frozen, finite and **synthetic** study: no actual equipment, clinical systems, ecological recovery or brain recordings were measured. This work is a deliberately small bridge between structural connectivity and the independently declared queue-service outcome. It is not a new graph-theory or queueing result.

## What was missing after RP-U004

RP-U004 measured repair of F1 and F2 — respectively, one source-target route and two internally vertex-disjoint routes — in a carefully controlled synthetic repair process. Its original function scores remain valid under its own graph-only definitions. Those booleans, however, do not describe the work left behind during a failure. The natural next question was therefore operational: **can the original graph be fully repaired while the declared workload has not recovered?**

This question already has a mature literature. Standard network recovery studies distinguish topology and delivery of demand; queue conservation and surplus-capacity clearing are classical. I am not claiming to discover them. Instead, the experiment forces the relational versus service distinction into one auditable finite set with direct event-level checks and small formal statements.

## The network, the perturbation and the measured function

There are exactly four vertices, with two two-edge routes from vertex 0 to vertex 3. The edge (1,3) is absent at t=0,1,2 and restored at t=3. Source-target **F1 is always true**, F2 is false during the break and true thereafter. The model, not an independent real sensor, maps these routes to one or two job-service slots per discrete tick. The queue starts empty, and every input job is individually accounted for.

Eight fully specified cases cross pre-repair arrivals of **0 or 2** unit jobs per tick with post-repair arrivals of **0, 1, 2 or 3** per tick. At each tick, the service delivers as many available queued jobs as slots allow. A functionally recovered service means **two consecutive observed end-of-tick empty queues**, not just a graph snapshot reporting two routes. Each case was independently computed as a direct integer recurrence and as an explicit FIFO unit-job event queue; all twelve ticks and all prefix conservation equations had to match exactly.

## The original complete eight-case census

The recovery timestamp below is the **second** of two qualifying end-of-tick observations, and null means **not observed through tick11** (right-censored within the simulation, not logically impossible forever).

| Pre-repair arrivals/tick | Post-repair arrivals/tick | First sustained empty-queue tick | Final backlog after tick11 | Total delivered / total arrived |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | 0 | 4 | 0 | 0 / 0 |
| 0 | 1 | **4** | 0 | 9 / 9 |
| 0 | 2 | 4 | 0 | 18 / 18 |
| 0 | 3 | None | 9 | 18 / 27 |
| 2 | 0 | 5 | 0 | 6 / 6 |
| 2 | 1 | **6** | 0 | 15 / 15 |
| 2 | 2 | None | 3 | 21 / 24 |
| 2 | 3 | None | 12 | 21 / 33 |

**Preregistered H1 supported in the finite model.** For the pre=0,post=1 and pre=2,post=1 worlds, all graph/repair/F1/F2/capacity trajectories and all *post-repair* incoming work are identical; the unobserved pre-repair queue history differs. The first world satisfies the two-sample empty-queue condition at tick4, the second at tick6, a **two-tick separation** meeting the fixed threshold. A topology-plus-future-incoming-work-only observer cannot uniquely deduce the recovery time in this simple model without the relevant historical/backlog state.

**Registered H2 also held in the finite model.** With pre=2,post=2, the graph returns to two routes at tick3 and thereafter serves two arrivals per tick. But it carries three queued jobs from the fault period; they remain queued at the end of **every** postrepair tick through t=11. This is the ordinary identity `(backlog + arrivals) - capacity = backlog` when arrivals equal capacity, not a new stochastic stability law. The clean pre=0,post=2 case is an important zero-backlog control, and the pre=2,post=0 case drains by tick5; post=3 cases grow their queues. Every expected negative control is included in the original archive and passed.

The original status, in the exact language of the frozen study, is:

**`FINITE_TOPOLOGY_INSUFFICIENT_FOR_RECOVERY_TIME`**.

This is **not** evidence that all network topologies fail to predict outcomes, nor does it test a full-state model with known queue contents and future arrivals. If the full state is known, the registered deterministic recurrence determines the whole future exactly. The limited conclusion is about the insufficiency of **the particular structural observation** used in this comparison.

## Formal reasoning and software auditing

The Lean module `RelationalPersistence/FunctionalBacklog.lean` proves six small facts/finite witnesses about this recurrence using `Mathlib` and without `sorry` or unproved axioms. In particular:
- matching intake and service preserve the backlog;
- capacity sufficient for arrivals keeps an already empty backlog empty;
- strict service surplus decreases every positive backlog;
- the registered three overloaded failed ticks create a queue of three;
- three surplus two-slot ticks remove the three queued tasks;
- three balanced two-slot ticks preserve them.

These claims are elementary, not priorities in the mathematics of queues. The Python experiment additionally checks two independently written graph/source-route criteria, paired independent queue implementations and prefix job conservation for all cases. It does **not** use Lean to certify every Python instruction or a physical network instrument. Results at multiple levels of evidence must not be conflated.

## What remains unknown and the next responsible experiment

The queue-server capacity was assigned from the graph's two routes; **it was not calibrated or measured from real equipment**. Arrival streams are imposed inputs, not empirical noise, and there is no real time-of-day clock, transmission latency, stochastic behavior, packet loss or independent physical health monitor. We have not identified a universal relational-persistence observable, explained cancer stabilization, verified a neural mechanism or derived the network model from PMFF primitives. Extending the same easy arithmetic to more toy worlds would not remedy these omissions.

A genuinely discriminating next step requires a separate prospective **service intervention** with externally logged arrivals, independently logged delivered work and queue state, a known repair event and synchronized clocks. Its principal target should be whether a model using the **measured residual state** predicts recovery better than a topology-only baseline on previously unexamined interventions. Such a study must not count this deterministic witness as an empirical replication.
