# RP-E002-A01 — First held-out observations: registered prediction fails

**Outcome: H0_not_rejected** under the frozen RP-E002-A01 rule.
This is a negative result for a **specific lightweight topology-aware
forecasting model**, not a proof that topology is irrelevant and not a
test of the physical scalar persistence hypothesis.

## Primary results

The following is **sensor-balanced MAE**, in the supplied data's
native units; lower values are better. The baseline column is the
stronger of last-observation persistence and training-only
weekday/time-slot historical mean. The permuted-graph model was
tuned independently on validation.

| Future horizon | Persistence | Seasonal | Permuted graph | Actual graph | vs. best temporal | vs. permuted graph | Both pass? |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 15 min | 3.46845 | 4.19571 | 3.45016 | 3.43541 | +0.9526% | +0.4274% | **No** |
| 30 min | 4.15897 | 4.19215 | 4.12211 | 4.11706 | +1.0078% | +0.1227% | **No** |
| 60 min | 5.32304 | 4.19398 | 5.26005 | 5.25923 | −25.3994% | +0.0156% | **No** |

Every horizon was declared to require **at least +1% improvement
against both** its strongest topology-blind baseline and the
separately tuned permuted-graph control. None met both conditions.

More precisely:

- **15 minutes:** the real graph narrowly improves persistence
  but misses the 1% threshold even before applying the permutation
  control. Its advantage over the shuffled graph is also below 1%.
- **30 minutes:** the graph exceeds 1% against persistence,
  but fails the graph-permutation control by a wide margin.
  One favorable comparison cannot override the preregistered
  conjunction.
- **60 minutes:** the seasonal time-only baseline is substantially
  better than the graph model, and the actual graph is almost
  indistinguishable from the permuted-graph forecast in MAE.

Primary evaluated speed pairs: **1,229,410** at 15 minutes;
**1,220,166** at 30 minutes; **1,207,094** at 60 minutes.
The graph lambdas chosen using validation alone were 0.05,
0.05 and 0.10 respectively. The permuted-graph lambdas
were 0.05 at every horizon.

This is the requested preregistered **negative finding**.
Do not retroactively tune parameters, remove 60 minutes,
change the gain threshold, drop the shuffled-network control
or relabel the negative outcome as success.

## Registered zero-inclusive sensitivity

A secondary analysis counts finite zeros as valid observations.
It reuses the **same validation-selected lambdas** rather than
retuning on held-out outcomes.

| Future horizon | Persistence MAE | Actual-graph MAE | Permuted-graph MAE |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 15 min | 4.05951 | 4.07558 | 4.10675 |
| 30 min | 5.38388 | 5.38742 | 5.40894 |
| 60 min | 7.43112 | 7.45429 | 7.42591 |

Under this alternative treatment the graph does **not**
outperform simple persistence at any horizon. This is a
robustness warning about missing/zero semantics, not a new
primary hypothesis test. In the original data, **8.11%** of
observations are zero, so assumptions about zeros matter
materially for any downstream inference.

## What this study cannot establish

METR-LA observes speeds. Its five-minute timestamp interval
does not measure physical response latency `tau`. The
sensor count is not physical capacity `B`, and raw variance
is not an independently measured noise variable `I`.
This archive also provides no independently verified labels
for disturbance onset and recovery times.

Consequently, **`B/(I*tau)` remains NOT IDENTIFIED**,
regardless of this forecast result. It would be incorrect to
claim either empirical confirmation or empirical refutation of
the scalar persistence conjecture based on A01.

The one-hop distance-weighted graph model is also **much
simpler than DCRNN** or a full spatiotemporal predictor.
A failure of this one model is not evidence that all
relational-topology forecasting methods fail. Stronger
baselines, additional data, uncertainty modeling and
independent event annotations would require a *new
preregistered study*, not an altered A01.
